# Korean broadcast music-rights clearance for export/OTT, and state of broadcast audio archives

Research date: 2026-11-04. Budget-limited (about 16 tool calls); web search was weak for Korean-specific sources. Labels: [VERIFIED] = stated by a primary or reputable source; [VENDOR] = vendor claim; [ANECDOTE] = fan/forum/interview; [ESTIMATE]; [HYPOTHESIS].

## KOMCA blanket licence scope and neighbouring-rights bodies

### Takeaway
One credible academic column (Electronic Times, 2022) states that KOMCA's licences for broadcast/film use are territorially limited to Korea and that a drama's music had to be replaced on export. I found no primary KOMCA document describing the broadcaster blanket contract's exact exclusions, and nothing on 음실련/음산협 requirements.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, opinion column by an IP-law professor, 2022-03-14] KOMCA's broadcast/film fee rules treat sync as part of the reproduction right, and licences are issued without asking creators which scenes are used; "license territory is limited to Korea". — [Electronic Times, Prof. Kim Kyung-sook](https://www.etnews.com/20220314000059)
- [VERIFIED as the author's account, 2022-03-14] The drama 천국의 계단 had its music ("Ave Maria") licensed via KOMCA for domestic broadcast; on export to Japan the song had to be replaced because the licence scope/territory did not extend overseas. The author says the issue was licence scope, not a missing licence. — [Electronic Times](https://www.etnews.com/20220314000059)
- [ANECDOTE/interview, 2024-10-14] Gaudio Lab's CEO cites a Ray Charles track in episode 1 of 김삼순: fine domestically, but exporting requires new royalties that can exceed the export revenue. Manually finding and inserting replacement music reportedly took about 3 weeks. — [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2024/10/14-373)
- [VERIFIED, 2020] OTT music-fee dispute: KOMCA proposed 2.5% of revenue; domestic OTTs proposed 0.625%; the Culture Ministry set 1.5%. This concerns domestic fee rates, not export clearance. — [Etoday](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/1973002), [Etnews 2020-08-17](https://www.etnews.com/20200817000090)
- [UNCONFIRMED press report, 2020] Netflix reportedly secures music IP when investing in content and gets 70-90% of music fees back from KOMCA. Secondhand; not verified. — [MTN, 2020-11-24](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2020112415380148315)
- [VERIFIED, 2026-08-27 per Asia Economy] KOMCA and Netflix signed a renewed licence covering 2021-2025 usage. — [Asiae English](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026082709162989173), [isplus](https://isplus.com/article/view/isp202608270191)
- [VERIFIED, 2026-02/03] Cue-sheet reporting is weak domestically: PPs do not submit cue sheets via BROMIS, so management ratios are set by the rights organisations. Domestic fee mechanics, not export. — [Asiae, 2026-03-13](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/life-general/2026031317522387997)
- [VERIFIED, 2022-02] 음산협 appointed Danal Entertainment as exclusive overseas distribution agent for its trust catalogue (music distribution, not broadcast sync). — [MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2022021611302991361)

### Inferences
- [HYPOTHESIS] Domestic blanket licences are Korea-scoped, so each export or global OTT sale needs fresh sync/master clearance or replacement; the 천국의 계단 example is consistent with this.
- [HYPOTHESIS] Netflix Originals avoid the problem by buying music buyouts up front, while licensed broadcaster catalogue is where replacement occurs.

### Gaps
- No KOMCA primary text (blanket contract clauses, territorial exclusion wording) found.
- No 음실련/음산협 requirement for export or OTT found (master/neighbouring rights for commercial recordings in dramas).
- No KOCCA report on 해외 유통 저작권 애로사항 retrieved; KOCCA pages found ([1812905_5425](https://kocca.kr/knowledge/publication/createcontents/1812905_5425.html)) were not read in full.

## Documented cases of BGM replacement

### Takeaway
Fan-wiki evidence confirms Netflix K-drama alterations where pre-existing songs lacking international rights are removed or swapped, but I found no documented case for 무한도전, 런닝맨, or 응답하라 and no press article on KBS/MBC archive VOD changes.

### Cited Findings
- [ANECDOTE, r/KDRAMA FAQ, 2024-05] Fan FAQ says songs altered on Netflix are often pre-existing tracks for which Netflix cannot or will not obtain international rights; examples listed include a song removed while dialogue remains, and a ringtone replaced with a generic one. — via search summary of [r/KDRAMA Netflix FAQ](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/KDRAMA/comments/1cldm3j/check_out_our_faq_netflix_faq_known_alterations) (direct fetch of the wiki mirror returned 403; not read in full)
- [ANECDOTE] Forum post on drama "Signal" argues rights cleared for original broadcast may not cover a worldwide Netflix release. — [MetaFilter](https://fanfare.metafilter.com/14708/Signal-Episode-1)
- [REPORTED, 2026-01, paid newsletter summary] A production company admitted using a drama OST without rights in a programme opening, replaced it, but past episodes on OTT replay still carried the music. Details need original-source check. — [Hankyung Magazine](https://magazine.hankyung.com/business/article/202405096558b) (search summary only, date and title uncertain)
- [VERIFIED as a practice] Radio replay services historically excluded music for copyright reasons. — search summary of [inews24](https://www.inews24.com/view/1425369) (not read)

### Inferences
- [HYPOTHESIS] The most likely pattern is quiet per-scene swaps on global releases; documentation lives in fan communities, not press.

### Gaps
- No evidence found for 무한도전, 런닝맨, 응답하라, KBS/MBC archive VOD music changes. Namu wiki was not reached. Targeted searches returned nothing; absence of results is not proof the changes did not occur.

## Cost and labour

### Takeaway
No Korean cost figure for clearance or re-mixing was found. The only Korean figures are vendor/interview claims (3 weeks manual vs 5 minutes AI) and a US per-song range.

### Cited Findings
- [ANECDOTE/VENDOR, 2024-10-14] Manual replacement about 3 weeks versus about 5 minutes with AI per the Gaudio Lab CEO; no prices disclosed. — [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2024/10/14-373)
- [US, secondary, date unclear] Popular-song clearance can run roughly $25,000-$50,000 per song; limited-term licences could be as low as 5% of perpetual cost. Per-song, not per-show; source quality low-to-medium. — [Book and Film Globe](https://bookandfilmglobe.com/television/on-streaming-classic-tv-shows-are-dropping-the-needle-drops/)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE/HYPOTHESIS] Labour is dominated by cue-sheet reconstruction plus music search, since domestic cue-sheet submission is weak.

### Gaps
- No KOCCA or 한국저작권위원회 cost data, no 재편집 cost, no cue-sheet management cost.

## Archive state (2-track masters, M&E, 클린본)

### Takeaway
Only vendor/interview statements support the claim that archives lack stems; I found no independent Korean survey or broadcaster statement.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, 2024-10-14] CEO says broadcasters generally hold only finished audio files, and about 99% of the world's content lacks separated sources; older works are the main focus. — [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2024/10/14-373)
- [VENDOR, 2026-01-09] Stems are "often lost or never archived due to tight schedules"; commercial film stems are hard to obtain. — [Gaudio Lab blog](https://www.gaudiolab.com/blog/231)
- [VERIFIED, industry practice] M&E is a standard deliverable for dubbing and international distribution. — [Netflix Backlot M&E guidelines](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/115006122187-M-E-Creation-Guidelines)

### Inferences
- [HYPOTHESIS] Older Korean programmes were mixed for domestic airing, so M&E/clean versions exist mainly where an international version was made on purpose.

### Gaps
- Nothing found on KBS/MBC/SBS archive digitisation projects, 국제판 M&E practice, or 클린본 delivery rules.
