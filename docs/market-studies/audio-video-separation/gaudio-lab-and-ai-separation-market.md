# Gaudio Lab's niche: separation sold as rights tooling

**Research date: 2026-11-04.** Label key used throughout: *verified fact* (primary page or reputable press), *vendor claim*, *anecdote* (fan, forum, interview), *estimate* (analyst, aggregator or arithmetic), *hypothesis* (inference). The original Slack conversation was not available to this report. The "Claims" section therefore tests the claims the research brief and notes refer to, and says so where it cannot check wording against the transcript.

Gaudio Lab sells a cloud workflow that splits a finished programme master into dialogue, music and effects (DME) and swaps the music for cleared tracks. It is a real, award-decorated product, but its public evidence of paying customers is thin. The strongest demand case is **music-rights clearance for export or OTT**, which has a documented Korean precedent and a CEO anecdote. "Distributors should already have stems" is only partly true: Netflix does require a fully filled M&E for new productions, so the gap is in legacy, third-party and broadcast-only masters, and that part rests mostly on vendor statements. The B2B field is small and moving: AudioShake raised $14M, Music.ai publishes $0.05/min for D/M/E stems, and Gaudio's own public price is $0.24/min. Pure consumer separation is a mature, free-bundled category, so none of the six app ideas looks like a strong standalone product, and the best of them is a creator tool for BGM clearance. For a small computer-vision team, the most defensible position is a human-in-the-loop, review-and-export workflow for rights-sensitive media, not a new separation model. 블러미 (BlurMe) is a face and licence-plate de-identification product sold to public bodies. Its flow of auto-detect, human review, export, bought through procurement is a useful template for such a workflow.

## What Gaudio Lab sells, what it has won, and how it earns

Gaudio Lab is a Seoul B2B audio-AI company founded 6 May 2015 under CEO Oh Hyun-oh. Its last public round was a **Series B of 11.3bn KRW in October 2021** led by LB Investment, with Samsung Venture Investment and Naver D2 Startup Factory taking part and cumulative funding reaching 16.9bn KRW (verified fact via an aggregator relaying 2021 press, [Nextunicorn](https://www.nextunicorn.kr/company/d6f20482bcb23a78)). No revenue, headcount or post-2021 round was found; THE VC and Nextunicorn hide current data behind a login ([THE VC](https://thevc.kr/gaudiolab)). Its older business licensed loudness and spatial-audio SDKs. Customers listed on the aggregator page include Naver, SKT, LG Electronics, CGV, FLO, Bugs and VIBE, with ">10 million daily users" (vendor claim, c. 2021, [Nextunicorn](https://www.nextunicorn.kr/company/d6f20482bcb23a78)).

The current headline product is **Gaudio Studio Pro (GSP)**, a cloud localisation suite for film and broadcast that works from a finished master with no separate D/M/E stems. Its modules are DME separation, music recommendation and replacement with timecodes and cue sheets, speech-to-text and subtitles, AI dubbing, and a mix/master step using its LM1 loudness tool to reproduce the original mix. Access is through an API (live), a Web Editor (waitlist) and an Expert Service run by its Wavelab studio (vendor claim, [GSP overview](https://www.gaudiolab.com/ko/products/gaudio-studio-pro-overview)). The **DME Separator** API launched in September 2025, and the **Music Replacer** draws on a library of more than 110,000 "human artist" tracks pitched as copyright-safe (vendor claim, same page). A self-serve Gaudio Developers platform opened on 1 October 2025 (vendor claim, [Gaudio news 216](https://www.gaudiolab.com/news/216), [audioXpress](https://audioxpress.com/news/gaudio-lab-launches-audio-ai-platform-for-developers)).

Awards are real but are product-submission honours rather than traction signals. The company reports four consecutive years of CES Innovation Awards and six honours in total: two in 2023 (LM1 and Gaudio Spatial Audio, per [audioXpress](https://audioxpress.com/tags/ott-services)), one in 2024 (CES lists Just Voice, [CES](https://ces.tech/innovation-awards/gaudio-just-voice), while Gaudio's blog credits GSEP; the attribution conflict is unresolved), one in 2025 for Music Placement (vendor press release, [startuprecipe](https://en.startuprecipe.co.kr/archives/pr-newswire/gaudio-lab-secures-ces-innovation-award-for-third-consecutive-year-ai-audio-technology-capturing-the-worlds-attention)), and two in 2026 for Gaudio Studio Pro, announced 5 November 2025 ([Gaudio news 216](https://www.gaudiolab.com/news/216), [audioXpress](https://audioxpress.com/news/gaudio-lab-wins-two-ces-2026-innovation-awards-for-gaudio-studio-pro-ai-audio-solution)). The arithmetic of 2+1+1+2 matches the "six" claim.

Revenue mechanics are only partly visible. The one public price is **$0.24 per track-minute for DME separation** (a newer cleaner M&E mode appears at $0.36/min in a search snippet), which implies roughly $14.40 per programme hour (vendor price, [Developer pricing](https://www.gaudiolab.com/developers/resources/pricing); arithmetic is an estimate). Web Editor and Expert Service prices are unpublished, so the likely big-ticket revenue is per-title services and enterprise or SDK licences (hypothesis). The GSP page shows logos for Disney, Wavve, SBS, Netflix and CJ ENM, but states no contract or scope, and Gaudio's own January 2026 blog names Disney and Netflix only as examples of studios that could benefit ([Gaudio blog 231](https://www.gaudiolab.com/blog/231)). Treat the logo list as unconfirmed. Further vendor claims: a 60-minute music replacement that took over a month finished in about three days with "up to 99% cost savings" (anonymous testimonial), and "twice the separation accuracy of competitors" with no published benchmark ([GSP overview](https://www.gaudiolab.com/ko/products/gaudio-studio-pro-overview), [news 216](https://www.gaudiolab.com/news/216)). Gaudio is a participant in the Ministry of Science and ICT "AI dubbing K-FAST" programme (8bn KRW total government investment, reported 20 August 2025; participation is verified, the ">90% faster" figure is a vendor claim) ([Unicorn Factory](https://www.unicornfactory.co.kr/article/2025082014561296717)).

## Why separation from a finished master is needed, ranked by evidence

The brief's four reasons differ sharply in how well they are supported. The table grades each, then the prose explains.

| Reason | Evidence grade | What backs it |
|---|---|---|
| M&E needed for dubbing and export | Strong as a requirement; weak as proof that stems are missing | Netflix specs (verified fact); vendor explainers |
| Music-rights clearance for OTT/export | Medium: one verified Korean precedent, one CEO anecdote, fan wikis | Etnews 2022 column; Byline 2024; r/KDRAMA |
| Archive masters lacking stems | Weak: vendor and CEO statements only | Gaudio blog, Byline |
| Korean broadcast single masters | Not evidenced independently | Nothing beyond the CEO's statement |

**M&E for dubbing is a hard requirement.** Netflix asks for a fully filled Music and Effects package as long-play discrete channels and says Foley is critical ([Netflix M&E guidelines](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/115006122187-M-E-Creation-Guidelines)). Titles mixed natively in 2.0 need a separate fully filled 2.0 M&E, and dubbing archival specs list a fully filled M&E ([Near Field 2.0](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/23506749090323-Near-Field-2-0-Stereo-Delivery-Specifications-and-Guidelines-for-Titles-Mixed-Natively-in-2-0), [archival specs](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002110287-Dubbing-and-Audio-Description-Archival-Asset-Specifications)). This is exactly why the objection "distributors should already have stems" has force for new Netflix-style productions. Netflix itself acknowledges a fully filled M&E may be unavailable and accepts DME splits or OMF/AAF in some contexts (snippet only, [Backlot](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/223351987)). A distributor-side vendor says missing M&E blocks foreign sales, that muting dialogue leaves "digital silence", and that removing sung vocals afterwards "usually results in a muddy, unusable mess" (vendor explainer, undated, [Travsonic](https://www.travsonic.com/mne-for-film-distribution/)). AudioShake's glossary says M&E is often missing for archival content (vendor claim, snippet only, [AudioShake](https://www.audioshake.ai/glossary/m-and-e-music-and-effects)).

**Music clearance is the best-supported Korean driver.** An IP-law professor's 2022 column states that KOMCA's broadcast and film licences are territorially limited to Korea, and that the drama 천국의 계단 had to replace "Ave Maria" on export to Japan because the licence scope did not extend overseas (verified as the author's account, [Electronic Times, 2022-03-14](https://www.etnews.com/20220314000059)). Gaudio's CEO cites a Ray Charles track in 김삼순: fine domestically, but export royalties could exceed export revenue, and manual replacement took about three weeks versus about five minutes with AI (anecdote and vendor claim, [Byline Network, 2024-10-14](https://byline.network/2024/10/14-373)). Fan wikis report Netflix removing or swapping pre-existing songs and ringtones (anecdote, [r/KDRAMA FAQ mirror](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/KDRAMA/wiki/kdramas_101/netflix_faqs)). The US parallel is well documented: WKRP, Scrubs and Dawson's Creek streaming versions swapped music because licences covered limited terms and media ([Book and Film Globe](https://bookandfilmglobe.com/television/on-streaming-classic-tv-shows-are-dropping-the-needle-drops/), secondary). What is missing is any primary KOMCA contract text, any 음실련/음산협 export rule, and any case for 무한도전, 런닝맨 or 응답하라. The KOMCA-Netflix disputes in the press concern domestic OTT royalty rates (KOMCA proposed 2.5%, domestic OTTs 0.625%, the ministry set 1.5%; [Etoday](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/1973002)), not export clearance, so they should not be cited for this point. A cost anchor: US popular-song clearance can run roughly $25,000 to $50,000 per song (low-to-medium quality secondary, [Book and Film Globe](https://bookandfilmglobe.com/television/on-streaming-classic-tv-shows-are-dropping-the-needle-drops/)).

**Archives and Korean single masters are the weakest links.** The CEO says broadcasters generally hold only finished audio and that about 99% of the world's content lacks separated sources ([Byline](https://byline.network/2024/10/14-373)); Gaudio's blog says stems are "often lost or never archived" ([blog 231](https://www.gaudiolab.com/blog/231)). Both are vendor statements. No broadcaster survey, KBS/MBC/SBS archive project or 국제판 M&E practice was found. The reasoning is plausible (domestic programmes were mixed for air, so M&E exists mainly when an international version was made on purpose) but remains a hypothesis. A buyer-side check with two or three archive or sales departments would settle it faster than further desk research. Domestic cue-sheet reporting is also weak (PPs do not submit cue sheets via BROMIS, [Asiae, 2026-03-13](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/life-general/2026031317522387997)), which supports the hypothesis that cue-sheet reconstruction is a large share of manual labour.

## B2B separation and AI dubbing: a thin field with public prices at five cents to two dollars

The separation vendors are few and some are unstable. **AudioShake** raised a $14M Series A announced 2025-10-01 (Shine Capital lead), claims 40+ enterprise partners (Disney Music Group, Warner Music Group, Universal Music, NFL Films, Warner Bros Discovery), "nearly 400% year-over-year revenue growth" off an undisclosed base, and 100M+ minutes processed; it publishes no API rates ([AudioShake](https://www.audioshake.ai/post/series-a-round-raise-14-million-shinecapital-origin-thomson-reuters), vendor claims; the round is a verified fact). Its named use for archives is removing unlicensed music from NFL Films footage. **Music.ai** (the B2B arm of Moises) raised a $40M Series A in January 2025 and publishes its rates ([Cultr](https://www.cultr.com/news/music-ai-moises-secures-40m-from-steve-aoki-3lau-others-in-series-a-round/)). A Portuguese-language report puts subscriptions at over 90% of its revenue (single secondary source, [Bloomberg Linea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com.br/startups/dona-do-moises-musicai-capta-us-40-milhoes-para-levar-ia-generativa-a-musicos/)). **iZotope RX** (Dialogue Isolate, Music Rebalance) changed hands: Boris FX acquired iZotope, reported 2026-07-02, after Native Instruments' preliminary insolvency in January 2026 ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/izotope-joins-boris-fx-exits-native-instruments/)). **Supertone** (HYBE) is being liquidated; shareholders resolved dissolution on 15 July 2026, with last-year revenue about 2.3bn KRW and a net loss about 13bn KRW ([Edaily, 2026-07-27](https://en.edaily.co.kr/news/eda202607275504/)). Supertone was a voice-AI company, not a music-sharing service. Its products were Shift (real-time voice changer), Clear (noise and voice isolation), Air, Play (TTS and voice creation), an API and Voice Builder. It also published the Supertonic TTS models that the Voice Life Companion app uses. Supertone's own notice says all services ended on 2026-08-31, and perpetual licences remain usable ([Supertone sunset](https://www.supertone.ai/ko/sunset)). A later report says Shift, Clear and Air are moving to Antinode Audio Inc. through a business transfer ([Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/hybe-announces-transfer-of-ownership-of-some-supertone-products-including-clear-to-antinode-audio-inc); checked 2026-11-04, not confirmed against a primary source). **Adobe** now ships on-device multi-stem "Enhance Audio" in Premiere beta, separating speech, music, ambience, effects and reverb ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/stem-separation-moves-into-the-edit-what-enhance-audio-changes-for-dialogue-workflows-in-premiere-beta/amp/)), and Steinberg SpectraLayers 12 offers AI unmixing for a one-time $349.99 ([Sonic State, 2025-07-04](https://sonicstate.com/news/2025/07/04/steinberg-releases-spectralayers-12)). Dolby's media-API docs now 301-redirect to OptiView; whether Enhance is discontinued is unconfirmed ([Dolby docs](https://docs.dolby.io/media-apis/docs/overview)). Audionamix shows no 2024-2026 news (a PitchBook "Out of Business" entry is ambiguous). The pattern is that separation is being absorbed into editors at low or zero marginal price, so standalone vendors compete on quality, rights-safe workflows and integrations (hypothesis).

## Consumer separation apps: success by registered users, awards and paying minutes

Moises is the one consumer winner by verifiable criteria. It reports **70M registered users at end-2025** (50M in January 2025; 30M in April 2023), 1.2B minutes processed and $50M+ raised, all vendor-reported ([Moises 2025 review](https://moises.ai/newsroom/company-milestones/2025-year-in-review), [MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-ai-raises-40m-in-series-a-round-as-its-moises-platform-hits-50m-users)). It won the Apple App Store Awards 2024 iPad App of the Year ([Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/apple-coloca-app-brasileiro-na-lista-dos-melhores-de-2024-veja-os-vencedores/)) and a Google Play "Best Personal Growth App" in 2021, which is a category award, not Best App overall ([Moises](https://moises.ai/blog/moises-news/best-personal-growth-app-google-play/)). It publishes **no revenue, subscriber count or download figure**; third-party revenue guesses run from $3.5M to $16.1M ARR and conflict (estimate, [ToolJunction](https://www.tooljunction.io/ai-tools/moises-the-musicians-app/company), [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/moises-revenue)). Registered users are cumulative sign-ups, not monthly actives, and the implied revenue at plausible conversion is far above those estimates, so the registered-user count is a loose metric.

LALAL.AI and vocalremover.org succeed on web traffic. A directory puts vocalremover.org near 9.5M monthly visits and LALAL.AI near 2.4M, and a Semrush snapshot of December 2025 shows LALAL.AI at 2.59M (estimates, [seektool.ai](https://seektool.ai/en/ai/vocalremover-org/alternatives), [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/vocalremover.org/competitors/)). LALAL.AI monetises with minute-metered plans: Lite $7.5/month, Pro $15/month, and top-ups from about $0.06/min (verified fact, [LALAL.AI](https://www.lalal.ai/pricing/)). **Apple Music Sing** shipped with iOS 16.2 in December 2022, requires an Apple Music subscription, and Apple said it uses an on-device model that processes music in real time on iPhone 11 and later ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/06/apple-music-is-getting-a-new-karaoke-like-feature-apple-sing), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/12/08/apple-music-sing-karaoke-compatible-devices)). It is a bundled subscriber perk with no public usage metric, so it cannot be called a standalone success. One competitor blog's claim that Apple serves pre-processed server-side stems is unverified ([StemSplit](https://stemsplit.io/blog/how-to-karaoke-apple-music)). The honest benchmark for the category is therefore millions of users and a plausibly profitable business in the low tens of millions of dollars, not a venture-scale outcome, unless the product attaches to a creator tool or licensed platform (hypothesis). No label or publisher suit against a consumer stem splitter was found, though that reflects a limited search; litigation has targeted generative music (Suno and Udio, June 2024, [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/06/24/record-labels-sue-ai-startups-copyright-infringement)).

Platform owners already give away the adjacent features. YouTube's Erase Song (updated July 2024) works only on content already claimed ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2902117?hl=en), [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/07/08/youtube-copyright-tool-ai-erase-song-content-id/amp/)). Prime Video Dialogue Boost (2023) uses a pre-computed server-side track ([Amazon](https://videocentral.amazon.com/support/delivery-specifications/dialogue-boost)). Samsung, Sony, LG and DTS ship dialogue boost in TVs and soundbars (vendor claims, e.g. [Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/au/support/tv-audio-video/active-voice-amplifier-on-samsung-tvs), [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/home-theater/dts-clear-dialogue)). CapCut markets free vocal removal with up to 10 stems (vendor claim, [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/resource/voice-isolator)).

### Feasibility of the six app ideas

The ranking below is a hypothesis built from those sources. Each idea's scoring weighs technical, legal, competitive and demand evidence.

| Rank | Idea | Verdict | Main reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vlog BGM clearance | Best consumer fit, needs a proof | Cross-platform gap beyond YouTube's claim-only tool; residual-music matching by Content ID is asserted only by vendor blogs ([Perso.ai](https://perso.ai/blog/youtube-copyright-claim-background-music)) |
| 2 | Music-education stems | Niche workflow play | Moises dominates; differentiate through teacher workflow, not separation |
| 3 | Shadowing player | Works only with licensed or YouTube content | DRM blocks OTT audio; incumbents such as Language Reactor work as subtitle overlays ([Mezzoguild](https://www.mezzoguild.com/language-reactor-review/)) |
| 4 | BGM-swap memes | A feature, not a product | CapCut, TikTok and Reels have libraries and templates |
| 5 | UGC dubbing challenge | Licensed catalogue only | Dubsmash shut on 2022-02-22 after Reddit's acquisition ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/23/reddit-is-shutting-down-dubsmash-and-integrating-video-tools-into-its-own-app), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-02-22/dubsmash-no-match-for-tiktok-dies-quietly-after-its-acquisition-by-reddit)); Korean fair use (Art. 35-5) is case by case ([korea.legal](https://korea.legal/wiki/%ec%a0%80%ec%9e%91%ea%b6%8c%eb%b2%95-%ec%a0%9c35%ec%a1%b0%ec%9d%985/)) |
| 6 | Clear-voice dialogue boost | Strong demand, wrong channel | Every TV maker plus Amazon and Apple ship it; OEM licensing is the realistic route |

Demand for the clear-voice problem is the best evidenced of all six: an NPR report of a survey says more than half of 1,260 Americans use subtitles because soundtracks are muddled (survey as reported, 2025-04-17, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/17/nx-s1-5346742/more-than-half-of-americans-use-subtitles-because-audio-is-muddled-survey-finds)). Its access problem is also the clearest: Android's playback-capture API requires user consent and lets source apps opt out ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/av-capture)), but whether Netflix, Prime Video or Disney+ actually block capture was not verified and must be tested on a device. The shadowing idea has a similar DRM caveat. Apple's tvOS Enhance Dialogue reportedly works with standard-player apps but not custom players such as Netflix (press, not rechecked on Apple's page, [iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/?p=960018)). The idea that survives is one where the user is also the rights-holder or has a licence: creators cleaning their own footage, teachers, or licensed catalogues.

## Video side: crowded enhancement, consolidating ad replacement, and why codec booths draw crowds

Person- and region-aware enhancement (restoring faces or text areas without identity drift) is plausible as a niche, but no source shows that buyers price it. The general category is crowded: Topaz Video AI is subscription-only with conflicting prices of $299/year versus $67/month (affiliate blogs, [Renderahouse](https://www.renderahouse.com/blog/topaz-ai-pricing), [MagicHour](https://magichour.ai/blog/topaz-video-ai-pricing)); NVIDIA RTX Video and TV chips from Samsung and LG ship AI upscaling (vendor claims, [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/rtx-video-sdk), [Samsung](https://news.samsung.com/sg/samsung-electronics-introduces-2026-ai-tvs-redefining-home-entertainment-with-smarter-experiences)); and analyst estimates for AI video upscaling software in 2025 run **$0.18B to $0.67B** (low-quality estimates, [Intel MR](https://www.intelmarketresearch.com/ai-video-upscaling-software-market-market-444), [WiseGuy](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/ai-video-upscaling-software-market)). Korean broadcast remastering is project work: SKT with Supernova and Daewon Broadcasting for anime, 4BY4's partnership with Japan's Resta, and Pixtree with SBS at BCWW 2024 ([SKT](https://news.sktelecom.com/181542), [KOCCA](https://www.kocca.kr/trend/vol35/sub/s21.html)). No contract values or per-minute prices were found, and no face-aware picture mode at Samsung or LG was confirmed.

Virtual advertising is the closest to the team's billboard-replacement experience and is consolidating. TGI Sport acquired Supponor (announced July 2024, terms undisclosed; Supponor claimed its AIR system was in 4,000+ live productions that year) after buying Brand Brigade ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2024/07/11/tgi-sport-acquires-supponor), [Sportcal](https://sportcal.com/news/tgi-sport-strengthens-virtual-ad-offering-with-supponor-acquisition)). ## Speaker diarization and separation: money sits in the application layer

Diarization is nearly a commodity. AssemblyAI charges **+$0.02 per hour** async and +$0.12 streaming (pricing file updated 2026-10-27, [AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/pricing.md)); pyannoteAI charges about €0.035 to €0.112 per hour ([pyannote](https://www.pyannote.ai/pricing)); Speechmatics bundles it (vendor claim, [Speechmatics](https://www.speechmatics.com/how-we-compare/elevenlabs-scribe-alternative.md)). At those rates, a million hours a year yields only about $20K to $120K (arithmetic from cited prices). The budgets are in products that need diarization: Otter passed **$100M ARR** in March 2025 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250325249430/en/Otter.ai-Breaks-$100M-ARR-Barrier-and-Transforms-Business-Meetings-Launching-Industry-First-AI-Meeting-Agent-Suite)), Gong reported above $500M ARR in May 2026 (aggregator reports of a company announcement, [SalesTechStar](https://salestechstar.com/?p=261992)), Abridge raised $300M at $5.3B in June 2025 ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/abridge-raises-300m-series-e-at-5-3b-valuation/)), and in Korea Daglo (ActionPower) closed a 6bn KRW Series B on 5 March 2026 with sales roughly quadrupling and B2B clients such as Hancom and Seoul National University Hospital ([Asia Economy](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026030511041441879)).

Accuracy is good on easy data and weak where it matters. An independent benchmark of 196.6 hours in five languages found pyannoteAI at **11.2% DER** and DiariZen at 13.3% as best open source, with Sortformer capped at four speakers ([arXiv 2509.26177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26177)). Errors rise with overlap, many speakers and short turns ([CHiME review, arXiv 2507.18161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18161)). Separation scores of 17 to 22 dB SI-SDRi come from clean synthetic mixtures (aggregator-sourced estimate, [HyperAI](https://hyper.ai/en/sota/tasks/speech-separation/benchmark/speech-separation-on-whamr)); real far-field results were not found, and no Korean diarization benchmark exists in the sources. For hearables, University of Washington research shows target-speech extraction in real time on embedded chips ([arXiv 2405.06289](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06289v3), [arXiv 2508.03047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03047?context=cs)), but buyers are a handful of OEMs with in-house teams. Broadcast, legal and forensic budgets could not be sized. The supportable conclusion is that a standalone diarization product is not a business, while a Korean on-prem or on-device pipeline for regulated sectors, or a published Korean overlap benchmark, are open hypotheses with no buyer evidence yet.

## Technology, run cost, on-device feasibility and licensing

**State of the art.** For music, band-split and mel-band RoFormer models and SCNet, trained with ZFTurbo's MIT-licensed MSST framework, lead the community MVSEP leaderboard at about **12.3 dB vocal SDR** (search-summary of MVSEP; verify live, [MVSEP](https://www.mvsep.com/algorithms)). Demucs is frozen: the repository was archived on 2025-01-01 and HT Demucs scores 9.0 dB on MUSDB18-HQ ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)). Cinematic DME is much harder. In the SDX23 challenge, on a hidden real-movie set, the best systems averaged about **8.1 dB with extra data and about 4.3 dB with DnR-only training**; extra data helped dialogue by about 6 dB, and DnR's music and effects stems contained vocals ([arXiv 2308.06981](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06981v4)). AudioShake placed second (8.077 dB) behind JusperLee (8.181 dB), so a "highest SDR" claim is unsupported. Netflix's DnR v3 adds 30+ languages because a model trained on v2 failed on non-English dialogue ([arXiv 2407.07275](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07275v2)). Meta's SAM Audio (paper arXiv 2512.18099, December 2025) offers text, visual and time-span prompts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18099)), and Dolphin (ICLR 2026) is a public-code audio-visual speech separator ([arXiv 2509.23610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23610v1)); audio-visual separation is where a computer-vision team has a natural edge (hypothesis).

**Run cost.** No independent RTF benchmark on a named GPU was found; vendor figures differ by about tenfold. They include about 30 s for a 4-minute track on an RTX 4090 (RTF about 0.125, [StemSplit](https://stemsplit.io/ja/blog/demucs-local-setup-guide)) and 116 ms per 7.8 s chunk on an RTX 3090 FP16 ([Demucs_v4_TRT](https://huggingface.co/MansfieldPlumbing/Demucs_v4_TRT)). Assuming RTF 0.01 to 0.13 and unverified GPU rental of $0.5 to $1.0 per hour, htdemucs costs roughly **$0.005 to $0.13 per audio hour**, and 2 to 4 times more for fine-tuned or RoFormer models, about $0.02 to $0.5 (estimate). Even the top of that range is far below Gaudio's $14.40 per hour list price, so compute is not the margin constraint; licensing, QC and labour are (hypothesis). A caveat: rental prices were not looked up, so benchmark before quoting.

**On-device.** Apple Music Sing is the mainstream precedent, and Algoriddim's djay used the Neural Engine for real-time stems by 2020 ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/20/06/18/apples-neural-engine-tapped-in-new-djay-pro-to-reinvent-live-mixing/)). HTDemucs CoreML and ONNX conversions exist for offline file processing ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/schism-audio/htdemucs-coreml)), but a BS-RoFormer export is 528 MB at fp32 ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/arraypress/stems-roformer)), so continuous mobile dialogue enhancement would likely need a smaller speech-specific model (estimate). AudioShake claims about 11 ms latency for live dialogue isolation (vendor claim, [AudioShake](https://www.audioshake.ai/post/audioshake-debuts-dialogue-rt-the-first-ai-dialogue-isolation-model-built-for-live-broadcast)). No independent phone or TV latency measurement was found. In practice, the OS and DRM access path, not compute, limits real-time processing of other apps' audio (hypothesis).

**Licensing.** Code is mostly permissive, but weights and data are the risk. Demucs code is MIT, yet the README says "the license of the weights is not clear" and that research-only MUSDB data was used ([GitHub](https://github.com/adefossez/demucs)). MUSDB18 is academic-only ([SigSep](https://sigsep.github.io/datasets/musdb.html)). MoisesDB is non-commercial ([GitHub](https://github.com/moises-ai/moises-db)). The SAM License covers code and weights as a royalty-free limited licence that does not mention commercial use directly, bans military use and reverse engineering, and needs counsel's reading ([LICENSE](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-audio/blob/main/LICENSE)). DnR v3 is CC BY-SA 4.0 with sources limited to public-domain and open-licence audio, the cleanest known open source for a commercial retrain, though ShareAlike reaching derived models is unsettled ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07275v2)). Whether a model trained on non-commercial data may be used commercially is legally unsettled (commentary, not ruling, [Black Duck](https://www.blackduck.com/blog/open-source-ai-model-licensing-risk.html)). AudioShake says it licenses production-library training data (vendor claim, [AudioShake](https://www.audioshake.ai/ai-ethics)). This is not legal advice. Community RoFormer weights (Kimberley Jensen, unwa, becruily) have undisclosed licences and provenance and should be treated as research-only. Separately, artifacts such as bleed, phasiness and "underwater" highs are documented by tutorial and vendor sources only, and I found no practitioner source on broadcast acceptability, so a human QC step should be assumed (hypothesis).

## Which wedges fit a small computer-vision team

The evidence favours workflow tools over model-building, for three reasons. First, separation models are commoditising (Adobe, SpectraLayers, free CapCut tools, $0.05/min Music.ai), and the SOTA is open-source MSST architectures, so model quality alone is not a moat. Second, the durable pain is rights risk, where the buyer already pays humans for slow work: Gaudio's CEO describes three weeks of manual music replacement per title. Third, the BlurMe template suggests review-centred products. The team's own history needs care here. The user later clarified that their earlier ad-panel replacement project aimed for real-time replacement and did not work technically, because of pose stabilization and blending (user statement, 2026-11-04). Photoreal region replacement is therefore a known hard area for this team, not a proven strength. Offline processing with masking or blurring and human review avoids most of it. Ranked by fit with the user's thesis (assist the user, help them make money, target tedious or high-turnover work), the hypotheses are these.

The first and strongest is a **rights-risk audit and replacement workbench for archive video**, with detection of music and brand/logo regions in finished masters, human review, a replacement or masking step, and cue-sheet or evidence export, sold per title to broadcasters, archive owners and distributors. It combines audio and video, which the notes say nobody confirmed doing together (hypothesis; Gaudio covers audio, Flawless covers visual dubbing). AudioShake's NFL Films use case (removing unlicensed music from archival footage) shows a buyer exists. It would integrate separation from AudioShake, Music.ai or Gaudio rather than compete with them. The main risk is that Gaudio already sells the audio half, and the evidence that Korean broadcasters actually hold only single masters is unverified, so the first step is a few buyer interviews rather than building.

The second is a **creator-side BGM clearance checker**, the top-ranked consumer idea, though it only works if separated output demonstrably passes Content ID, which no independent test shows, and YouTube and CapCut cap the price. The third is **audio-visual speaker separation for hearables or meetings**, using face tracking plus a Dolphin-class model; it fits the CV background but sells to a few OEMs through long licensing cycles, and the Dolphin and training-data licences must be cleared first. Wedges to avoid on this evidence are generic diarization, consumer stem apps, UGC dubbing hosting, and standalone dialogue-boost apps. A visibly acquirable path is also supported: strategics bought Deep Render, iSIZE and Supponor, and iZotope changed hands to Boris FX (all verified above), which suggests post-production and streaming-delivery technology firms buy narrow, working technology, though no source shows an engineering or simulation firm buying audio-video tools, so that remains the user's hypothesis to test.

### Self-serve entry product (hypothesis, to validate)

3. The user reviews the flagged spans.

- Self-hosting later costs roughly $0.005–0.5 per audio hour (see the run-cost section), but needs model weights that are cleared for commercial use.

**The key risk, and a cheap test.** Does separated or "cleaned" audio still match in Content ID or other recognizers (Audible Magic)? No independent evidence was found either way. A one- to two-day experiment settles it:
1. Take ten of the user's own videos with commercial music audible in the background.
2. Process them with two or three separation tools.
3. Upload them as unlisted videos.
4. Count the claims.

Run this before building.

**Other open items.**
- A music source for replacement that a SaaS may redistribute: a licensed library partner, commercially licensed AI music, or a removal-only v1.
- The licences of the separation weights used.
- Whether platform features close the gap first, for example YouTube Erase Song, which today works only on already-claimed videos.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "can AI separate audio?" to "who pays to resolve rights and delivery gaps in finished media, and who owns the review step?". Separation quality is converging on open-source architectures and public prices as low as $0.05 per minute, and compute is a small cost line, so the margin sits in the surrounding workflow: cue sheets, replacement, QC, contract and distribution acceptance. The most credible demand is music-clearance for export, and the weakest is the claim that Korean archives uniformly lack stems, which one round of buyer interviews could confirm or kill.

For this team, the unusual asset is the pairing of video understanding with audio rights work, because the BlurMe pattern (legal trigger, tedious manual task, human review, on-prem option, procurement route) and the virtual-advertising consolidation both show that small vision companies find exits when they solve one narrow, rule-driven replacement job. The key unknowns, which the user should close before building, are named-customer evidence for Gaudio Studio Pro, the real share of Korean programmes without M&E, whether OTT apps block Android playback capture, and the licence status of any open weights the pipeline would use.
