# Geomagic Software Product Line (Design X, Control X, Wrap, Freeform, for SOLIDWORKS): Business and Financial History (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-03. All dates are explicit. "Disclosed" means company filings or press releases. "Derived" means my arithmetic on disclosed numbers. "Estimate" means a third-party model (low confidence).

Correction to the brief: the sale to Hexagon was announced **December 12, 2024**, not "around 2025". It closed **April 1, 2025** (3D Systems' date) and Hexagon announced the closing on April 2, 2025.

---

## 1. What revenue figures have been disclosed for the Geomagic software business, and what were the Hexagon deal terms?

### Takeaway
The only official full-year revenue figure is Hexagon's: **about $30M of revenue and about 100 employees in 2023**, with operating margins **higher than** Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence's (roughly 25-27%). 3D Systems' CEO said Geomagic brought in **about $7M in Q1 2025**, a run-rate of about $28M a year. The price was **$123M, cash- and debt-free**. 3D Systems received **$119.4M in cash** after adjustments and booked a **$125.7M pre-tax gain**.

### Cited Findings
**Deal announcement (December 12, 2024)**
- Hexagon announced on 2024-12-12 that it would buy the Geomagic software business from 3D Systems for **$123M on a cash and debt free basis**. In 2023 the business had **"around 100 employees, generated revenues of around $30M"** and "delivered operating margins accretive to those of the Manufacturing Intelligence division". Closing was expected in early Q2 2025, subject to regulatory approval — [Hexagon press release, 2024-12-12](https://hexagon.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2024/hexagon-acquires-inspection-and-3d-modelling-software-capabilities-from-3d-systems); the same figures are repeated by [Nasdaq/Zacks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/3d-systems-sell-geomagic-software-portfolio-hexagon-123-mln-deal) and [Geo Week News](https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/hexagon-acquires-inspection-and-3d-modelling-software-capabilities-from-3d-systems/)
- 3D Systems' own release (Rock Hill, SC, 2024-12-12) names the products sold: **Design X, Control X, Freeform, Wrap and Geomagic for SOLIDWORKS**. It expected closing in H1 2025 and **disclosed no revenue, EBITDA or headcount**. 3D Systems kept 3D Sprint, 3DXpert and the Oqton Industrial Manufacturing OS — [3D Systems press release](https://www.3dsystems.com/press-releases/3d-systems-announces-sale-geomagicr-software-portfolio)
- At the time, Hexagon described itself as having about 24,500 employees in 50 countries and net sales of about EUR 5.4bn — [Geo Week News](https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/hexagon-acquires-inspection-and-3d-modelling-software-capabilities-from-3d-systems/)

**Closing (April 2025)**
- 3D Systems says the sale closed **2025-04-01**. It received **$119.4M in cash, "which reflected applicable purchase price adjustments"** under the Asset Purchase Agreement and Business Transfer Agreement. Geomagic sat in the **Industrial Solutions** segment — [3D Systems 10-Q Q1 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000091063825000010/ddd-20250331.htm)
- Hexagon announced on 2025-04-02 that the Geomagic suite "will be consolidated as of April 2025 and will operate within Hexagon's Manufacturing Intelligence division". About **EUR 40M** of items affecting comparability was expected, covering transaction, overlapping-technology and integration costs. That figure is a combined bucket for Geomagic, Septentrio NV and the planned "NewCo" (Asset Lifecycle Intelligence) separation, so it is not Geomagic-only — [Hexagon/PR Newswire, 2025-04-02](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hexagon-completes-the-acquisitions-of-the-geomagic-software-suite-and-septentrio-nv-302418070.html)
- 3D Systems' CEO said **"approximately $100 million of net proceeds"** would reach the balance sheet. That is below the $119.4M gross cash, presumably after taxes and fees, but no reconciliation was found — [Metal AM](https://www.metal-am.com/3d-systems-completes-sale-of-geomagic-software-portfolio-to-hexagon-for-123-million/); [3D Printing Industry, 2025-04-08](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/geomagic-suite-now-officially-integrated-into-hexagon-238206/)

**Gain and balance sheet (SEC filings)**
- Pre-tax **gain on the sale of Geomagic: $125.7M**, booked in Q2 2025. The Q1 10-Q had estimated about $125M, or $125.3M in the MD&A — [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000162828026015997/ddd-20251231.htm); [10-Q Q2 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000091063825000016/ddd-20250630.htm); [10-Q Q1 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000091063825000010/ddd-20250331.htm)
- **Geomagic held-for-sale disposal group** at 2024-12-31 (and 2025-03-31), in $ thousands — [10-Q Q1 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000091063825000010/ddd-20250331.htm):
  - Accounts receivable: 765 (533)
  - Intangibles, net: 917 (801)
  - Other assets: 1,447 (1,559)
  - **Total assets: 3,176 (2,936)**
  - **Deferred revenue: 7,197 (7,190)**
  - Other liabilities: 2,260 (2,322)
  - **Total liabilities: 10,251 (10,305)**
  - "No loss was recognized" on remeasurement. Geomagic was not treated as discontinued operations because it was "not a strategic shift"
- The filings **do not disclose Geomagic's standalone revenue**, and the FY2025 10-K does not put a dollar figure on the divestitures' revenue impact. It says only that Industrial Solutions services revenue fell $9.8M "primarily due to the impact of divestitures", and that Industrial Solutions segment revenue fell from **$250.4M (2024) to $207.3M (2025), down 17.2%** — [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000162828026015997/ddd-20251231.htm)

**Quarterly Geomagic revenue from earnings calls (2025)**
- On the Q2 2025 call, CEO Jeff Graves said the Geomagic divestiture "**contributed roughly $7 million of revenue in Q1**" 2025. CFO Jeff Creech said total revenue was "down 16% year-over-year or 11% when excluding Geomagic" and Industrial Solutions "declined 23% or 13% excluding Geomagic". Gross margin was 39% reported and 38% excluding Geomagic. Graves called Geomagic "the non-core Geomagic software platform" — [Q2 2025 call transcript (VectorShift copy; third-party transcript)](https://vectorshift.ai/research/companies/3d-systems/earnings/2025-q2.html)
- Q3 2025: revenue was $91.2M, down 19% reported and about 14% excluding Geomagic. Industrial Solutions was about $48M, **down 16% reported and 4.5% excluding Geomagic** — [Q3 2025 call summary (VectorShift)](https://vectorshift.ai/research/companies/3d-systems/earnings/2025-q3-summary.html); the headline -19% is confirmed in the [Q3 2025 8-K earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000162828025048900/a3d2025-09x30earningsrelea.htm)

### Inferences
- **Derived Q3 2024 Geomagic revenue of about $6.9M**, using only the Q3 numbers above. Q3 2024 Industrial Solutions = 48 / 0.84 ≈ $57.1M. Q3 2024 Industrial Solutions excluding Geomagic = 48 / 0.955 ≈ $50.3M. The difference is about $6.9M. This matches the "$7M in Q1 2025" remark. **2024 Geomagic revenue was therefore probably about $27-28M**, a little below the ~$30M Hexagon cited for 2023. That points to flat to slightly declining revenue going into the sale.
- **The gain exceeding the cash shows the business was carried at about zero or less on 3D Systems' books.** The disposal group had about $7M of net liabilities, mostly deferred revenue, and under $1M of intangibles. Cash of $119.4M plus about $7M of net liabilities assumed by the buyer, less costs, gives roughly the $125.7M gain. 3D Systems had effectively written off the 2012-2013 acquisition goodwill and intangibles (about $87M paid).
- **Implied profitability.** Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence's operating margin was 26.7% in Q2 2024 and 24.8% in Q2 2025 ([Hexagon Q2 2025 presentation](https://airtools-hexagon.prod-mid-euw3.investis.com/sites/airtools-hexagon/files/pr/hexagon_q2_2025_presentation.pdf)). "Accretive" therefore implies Geomagic's EBIT margin, on Hexagon's definition and probably excluding some 3D Systems corporate allocations, was **above about 25%**, or at least about $7.5M of EBIT on $30M of revenue. That is a cash-generative, mature software asset.
- **Relative size inside Hexagon.** About $7M a quarter (roughly EUR 6-6.5M) is about 1.3% of Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence's Q2 2025 revenue of EUR 487.5M ([Hexagon Q2 2025 presentation](https://airtools-hexagon.prod-mid-euw3.investis.com/sites/airtools-hexagon/files/pr/hexagon_q2_2025_presentation.pdf)). Geomagic is a tuck-in, and Hexagon is unlikely ever to report it separately.

### Gaps
- No disclosed EBITDA, ARR or customer count for Geomagic. No breakdown by product (Design X vs Control X vs Wrap/Freeform). No separate figure for 2024 revenue.
- The ~$100M vs $119.4M net-proceeds gap is not reconciled in the sources found.
- The Q2/Q3 2025 call quotes come from a third-party transcript site (VectorShift), not the official 3D Systems IR transcript.
- No Hexagon interim report found that quantifies Geomagic's acquired-revenue contribution. Hexagon reports a combined "structure" effect only.

## 2. What is the revenue trend over time (growth, flat, decline)? Recurring vs perpetual mix? Split by product?

### Takeaway
The data **supports the "steady, flat revenue" claim, and in real terms the business has shrunk.** When 3D Systems bought the two parts in 2012-2013, they were expected to add roughly $15M (Rapidform) and about $17M for ten months (Geomagic) of revenue, about $32-35M combined. A decade later the combined business made about $30M (2023) and about $27-28M (2024 run-rate). Nominal revenue has been flat to down for about 11 years, while the 2026 releases look incremental (hardware support, file-format updates). 3D Systems never disclosed Geomagic revenue year by year, so this is an endpoint comparison, not a series.

### Cited Findings
**Starting point (2012-2013)**
- Rapidform: 3D Systems said its acquisition of Rapidform (INUS Technology, Seoul) on 2012-10-09 would add about **$15M in revenue**. It is not stated whether that is an annual figure — [Design News](https://www.designnews.com/design-hardware-software/3d-systems-bulks-scanning-piece-its-3d-vision); 3D Systems called it "not significant" and reported its revenue within printers and other products — [3D Systems 10-Q Q3 2012](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000910638/000119312512434608/d426213d10q.htm)
- Geomagic: completed 2013-02-27. 3D Systems expected Geomagic to add about **$17M of revenue to 2013 guidance**, covering roughly ten months of ownership — [3D Systems 8-K press release, 2013](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000117184313000787/newsrelease.htm); [GfxSpeak](https://gfxspeak.com/archives/3d-systems-pays-55-million-for-geomagic/)
- INUS Technology issued a release titled "INUS Technology announces H1 2010 results – revenue up more than 50%". It was once hosted on 3dsystems.com and now returns 404, so the figures could not be retrieved — [former URL](https://3dsystems.com/press-releases/inus-technology-announces-h1-2010-results-revenue-up-more-than-50)

**End point (2023-2025)**
- About **$30M revenue in 2023**, about 100 employees — [Hexagon, 2024-12-12](https://hexagon.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2024/hexagon-acquires-inspection-and-3d-modelling-software-capabilities-from-3d-systems)
- About **$7M in Q1 2025** — [Q2 2025 call (VectorShift)](https://vectorshift.ai/research/companies/3d-systems/earnings/2025-q2.html)

**Context: 3D Systems' total software revenue (includes Geomagic plus Cimatron, GibbsCAM, 3DXpert, 3D Sprint and others; not Geomagic-only)**
- Total software revenue (products plus services) was **$101.2M in 2018** and **$98.5M in 2019** (15.7% of 3D Systems revenue), down 2.7% — [3D Systems 10-K FY2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000091063820000010/ddd-20191231.htm)
- Software revenue within products was **$53.7M in 2019** and **$43.7M in 2020**, a COVID-affected year. That is a different basis from the figures above — [3D Systems 10-K FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000091063821000008/ddd-20201231.htm)
- In 2022, 3D Systems moved Geomagic under a separate software division led by Oqton, a recent acquisition — [Hexagon blog, "History of Geomagic software"](https://blog.manufacturing.hexagon.com/digital-transformation-in-manufacturing/reverse-engineering/history-of-geomagic-software/)

**Recurring revenue signal**
- Deferred revenue in the Geomagic disposal group was **$7.197M** (current) at 2024-12-31, plus $2.26M of "other liabilities", which may include long-term deferred revenue — [10-Q Q1 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000091063825000010/ddd-20250331.htm)
- Pricing as of January to October 2026 points to a push toward subscriptions and tiers. Design X is sold in **Go / Plus / Pro** tiers. A reseller copy of Hexagon's January 2026 USD price book lists Go at about $1,900 a year, Plus at about $4,190 a year, and Pro at about $7,280-9,460 a year depending on activation type. Control X Premium is about $5,520 a year (up to $7,176 by activation option), and a USB dongle costs $550 — [GoMeasure3D Control X subscription listing](https://shop.gomeasure3d.com/products/geomagic-control-x-annual-subscription-license); [GoMeasure3D Design X listing](https://shop.gomeasure3d.com/products/geomagic-design-x); [Hexagon Design X plans page](https://hexagon.com/es/products/geomagic-design-x/geomagic-design-x-plans); [Rev1Tech](https://rev1tech.com/shop/software/reverse-engineering/geomagic-design-x). These are reseller prices and vary by source.

**Technical progress (qualitative, 2026)**
- **Design X 2026.1** adds LiveTransfer support for SOLIDWORKS 2026, import up to Creo 12.0 and SOLIDWORKS 2026, direct import of Leica .LGSx files, DefinSight (Scantech) support, RDS 6.6 grayscale line scanning, and Split Mesh upgrades — [Hexagon "What's new in Design X"](https://hexagon.com/products/geomagic-design-x/whats-new)
- **Control X 2026.1** was announced 2026-06-24 and covers expanded scanner integration (ZG handheld scanners, HyperProbe, a DefinSight plugin replacing ScanViewer), more automation and scripting actions, boundary inspection, and performance work. No AI features were announced — [Quality Digest, 2026-06-24](https://www.qualitydigest.com/inside/manufacturing-news/hexagon-announces-geomagic-control-x-20261-062426.html); [Hexagon "What's new in Control X"](https://hexagon.com/products/geomagic-control-x/whats-new); [Metrology News](https://metrology.news/geomagic-control-x-2026-1-advances-scan-based-inspection-and-metrology-automation/)

### Inferences
- **Verdict on the user's hypothesis:**
  - **Flat revenue: verified, and arguably a slight nominal decline.** About $32-35M expected at acquisition (2012-13), about $30M in 2023, about $27-28M in 2024 and Q1 2025 run-rate. Cumulative US CPI from 2013 to 2024 was roughly 35%, so real revenue fell by roughly a quarter to a third.
  - **Scope caveat.** The 2012-13 base included things that may not have been in the 2024 sale perimeter: SensAble haptic hardware (Touch devices), dental and Align-related work, and the legacy Geomagic Studio and Qualify lines. Some of the apparent stagnation may be scope change.
  - **Little technical progress: consistent with the evidence, though qualitative.** The 2026.1 releases are mostly compatibility, file-format and scanner-driver updates.
- **Recurring share (rough inference, low confidence).** If maintenance and subscriptions are billed annually in advance and recognized ratably, a $7.2M current deferred revenue balance suggests annual recurring billings of very roughly $10-15M, or about 35-50% of a $28-30M business. The rest would be perpetual licenses and hardware-bundled seats. The "steady" quality of the revenue fits a large maintenance base.
- **Geomagic's share of 3D Systems software.** About $30M of roughly $100M of 3D Systems software revenue in 2018-2019, about 30%, assuming Geomagic was about $30M then. The rest was largely Cimatron/GibbsCAM, sold in 2021.
- A ~$7M/quarter run-rate across Q3 2024 and Q1 2025 shows a stable quarterly cadence with no visible growth.

### Gaps
- No year-by-year Geomagic revenue for 2013-2022 was found. 3D Systems never broke it out.
- No split between Design X, Control X, Wrap, Freeform and for SOLIDWORKS. Industry lore puts Design X and Control X as the bulk, but I found no source.
- No disclosed split between recurring and perpetual revenue. The Rapidform "$15M" figure may not be an annual run-rate.
- Hexagon has not disclosed post-acquisition Geomagic growth.

## 3. What price-to-revenue multiple did the deal imply, and how does it compare with other 3D and metrology deals?

### Takeaway
**About 4.1x 2023 revenue** ($123M / ~$30M), or about 4.4x the implied 2024 run-rate. On the "accretive" margin, that is **roughly 15x EBIT or less**. This is a typical price for a mature, profitable, low-growth niche software asset. It is above the hardware-heavy metrology comps (Creaform about 2.3x, FARO about 2.7x) and far above 3D Systems' distressed Oqton/3DXpert exit. Price/revenue on 3D Systems' 2012-13 outlay was about 2.6x.

### Cited Findings
- **Geomagic to Hexagon (announced 2024-12, closed 2025-04):** $123M, cash and debt free, on about $30M 2023 revenue and about 100 employees — [Hexagon](https://hexagon.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2024/hexagon-acquires-inspection-and-3d-modelling-software-capabilities-from-3d-systems)
- **3D Systems' original cost:**
  - Rapidform: $35M cash (2012-10), or $33.9M net of cash acquired — [3D Systems 8-K 2012](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000910638/000117184312003600/exh_991.htm); [3D Systems 10-K FY2012](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000910638/000119312513072822/d491966d10k.htm)
  - Geomagic: $55M cash (closed 2013-02-27), or $52.7M net — [3D Systems 8-K 2013](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000117184313000787/newsrelease.htm); [3D Systems 10-Q Q1 2013](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000910638/000119312513184477/d527751d10q.htm)
- **Creaform to AMETEK (2013-10):** about $120M for a handheld 3D scanner and portable CMM maker with about **$52M annual sales**, roughly 2.3x sales and hardware-dominated — [Manufacturing.net](https://www.manufacturing.net/industry40/news/13094055/ametek-acquires-creaform-for-120-million); [AMETEK 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001037868/000129993313001882/exhibit1.htm)
- **FARO to AMETEK (closed 2025-07-21):** $44.00 per share, about **$920M** net of cash, for about **$340M** annual sales, roughly 2.7x sales; hardware plus software — [AMETEK press release](https://www.ametek.com/newsroom/news/investor/2025/july/ametek-completes-acquisition-of-faro-technologies); [AMETEK 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1037868/000103786825000053/exhibit991faroclosing72125.htm)
- **GOM to ZEISS (2019):** price **undisclosed**. GOM had about **EUR 150M** in sales — [ZEISS press release](https://www.zeiss.com/corporate/en/about-zeiss/present/newsroom/press-releases/2019/acquisition-of-gom.html); [optics.org](https://optics.org/news/zeiss-expands-metrology-portfolio-with-gom-acquisition)
- **Cimatron and GibbsCAM to Battery Ventures (3D Systems; announced 2020-11-03, closed 2021-01-01):** $65M headline, about $64.2M after adjustments and excluding $8.9M of cash. They were combined with SigmaTEK into CAMBRIO — [Nasdaq/3D Systems press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/3d-systems-announces-sale-of-cimatron-and-gibbscam-businesses-2020-11-03); [Metal AM](https://www.metal-am.com/3d-systems-completes-sale-of-cimatron-and-gibbscam-businesses/); [Schnitger Corp](https://schnitgercorp.com/2020/11/03/battery-ventures-buys-cimatron-gibbscam-from-3d-systems/)
- **Oqton and 3DXpert to Hubb Global Holdings (closed 2025-10-31):** $3.3M cash plus a revenue-based royalty receivable with a present value of $7.1M, about $10.4M in total — [3D Systems 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000162828026015997/ddd-20251231.htm)

### Inferences
- **Multiples (derived):**
  - $123M / $30M = **4.1x** 2023 revenue
  - $123M / ~$27.6M (4 × $6.9M) = **about 4.5x** the 2024 run-rate
  - $119.4M cash / $30M = 4.0x
- **EV/EBIT.** If EBIT margin is at least 25%, EBIT is at least $7.5M and EV/EBIT is **16x or less**. That is moderate for profitable software and is consistent with buying a no-growth asset for cash flow and portfolio fit (scan-to-CAD and inspection for Hexagon's scanners) rather than for growth.
- **Return for 3D Systems:** $123M against about $87M of cash paid in 2012-13, about 1.4x over 12 years (roughly 3% a year nominal, before the cash flows earned in between). A modest outcome that again points to a flat business.
- Pure software usually commands higher multiples than hardware-heavy metrology (Creaform 2.3x, FARO 2.7x). Geomagic's 4x sits above those but well below growth-software multiples (often 8x+ revenue). That is consistent with a market that viewed it as low- or no-growth.
- 3D Systems' 2020-2025 software exits form a pattern of non-core asset sales to repair the balance sheet: Cimatron/GibbsCAM for $65M, Geomagic for $123M, Oqton/3DXpert for about $10M. Geomagic was the most valuable.

### Gaps
- No revenue figure found for Cimatron/GibbsCAM at sale, so no multiple can be computed.
- Hexagon's other Manufacturing Intelligence acquisitions (MSC Software, Vero, AICON, Q-DAS, Volume Graphics, CADS Additive and others) were not priced in this session.
- **Artec 3D:** no acquisition found; it appears to remain independent, unverified. **InnovMetric (PolyWorks):** no transaction found; believed privately held, unverified.

## 4. Corporate history: Raindrop Geomagic, INUS/Rapidform, 3D Systems, the Seoul team, and Hexagon since the acquisition

### Takeaway
Three lines of descent:
- **Raindrop Geomagic:** Illinois 1996, then North Carolina from 1999. Products: Studio, Qualify and Wrap.
- **SensAble:** Freeform and haptics, acquired by Geomagic in 2012.
- **INUS Technology / Rapidform:** Seoul 1998. Products: XOR, XOV and XOS.

3D Systems bought Rapidform (October 2012) and Geomagic (February 2013), merged the lines, and relaunched them as **Design X and Control X**, mainly built on the Rapidform XOR and XOV code. The blog dates this to 2016, but the Design X name existed earlier. In 2022 the products moved under the Oqton-led software unit. They were sold to Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence in April 2025. Since then Hexagon has kept the Geomagic brand, promised continued investment and partner support, moved to tiered subscriptions, and shipped 2026.1 releases. **I found no public information on what happened to the Seoul development team or on Geomagic-specific layoffs.**

### Cited Findings
**Origins**
- Hexagon's history blog gives this timeline — [Hexagon blog](https://blog.manufacturing.hexagon.com/digital-transformation-in-manufacturing/reverse-engineering/history-of-geomagic-software/):
  - 1993: Thomas Massie and Kenneth Salisbury found SensAble (haptics, which led to Freeform)
  - 1996: Ping Fu and Herbert Edelsbrunner found Raindrop Geomagic in Illinois (point cloud to mesh, the root of Wrap)
  - **1998: alumni of a university CAD lab found INUS Technology in Seoul**
  - 1999: Geomagic Studio launches
  - 2001: Geomagic Qualify launches; INUS launches Rapidform
  - **2007: INUS reorganizes into XOR (reverse engineering), XOV (inspection) and XOS (scan data prep)**, with the parametric history-based approach developed around this time
  - 2012: Geomagic buys SensAble's 3D design business; 3D Systems then buys Rapidform and Geomagic
  - **2016: Geomagic Control X and Design X launched after 3D Systems merged the Rapidform and Geomagic products**
  - 2022: Geomagic moved under the Oqton-led software division
- Ping Fu became CEO in 1997 and moved the company from Illinois to North Carolina in 1999. Sales tripled between 2001 and 2003. The company nearly went bankrupt under a hired CEO, then recovered under Fu with a significant Align Technology contract. Fu became 3D Systems' Chief Strategy Officer after the February 2013 sale — [Wikipedia: Ping Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_Fu)
- Rapidform acquisition: 3D Systems announced on 2012-10-09 that it had bought Rapidform (Seoul), "a provider of 3D scan-to-CAD and inspection software tools", for $35M cash — [3D Systems 8-K 2012](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000910638/000117184312003600/exh_991.htm); [Design News](https://www.designnews.com/design-hardware-software/3d-systems-bulks-scanning-piece-its-3d-vision)
- A Korean job feature on INUS Technology describes it as a 3D scanning and reverse-modelling software firm whose flagship is Rapidform, hiring software developers, Java developers, QA engineers and technical writers. The article is undated — [News Tomato](https://www.newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=164842)

**Hexagon since April 2025**
- Josh Weiss, President of Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence, said: "We will continue to support all hardware and software partnerships for Geomagic customers" and "We will invest in developing and supporting this software with our considerable expertise and IP"
- Stated plans:
  - Add Hexagon's GD&T libraries to Control X
  - Direct scanning support for Hexagon hardware (SmartScan VR800, ATLASCAN Max, MARVELSCAN)
  - Keep CAD and PMI import from CATIA, Creo, Inventor, NX and SOLIDWORKS
- Source for the above: [3D Printing Industry, 2025-04-08](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/geomagic-suite-now-officially-integrated-into-hexagon-238206/). - **Branding:** the products are still sold as "Geomagic Design X" and "Geomagic Control X" on hexagon.com. ## 5. Did 3D Systems divest other software at the same time? Who bought it and for how much?

### Takeaway
Yes. **Oqton (manufacturing OS) and 3DXpert (metal additive manufacturing software) went to Hubb Global Holdings, LLC.** The deal was agreed in September 2025 and closed on **2025-10-31** for **$3.3M cash plus a royalty with a present value of $7.1M**, about $10.4M in total, producing a **$13.9M gain**. Earlier, **Cimatron and GibbsCAM** went to Battery Ventures (CAMBRIO) for **$65M** (closed 2021-01-01).

### Cited Findings
- Oqton and 3DXpert to Hubb Global Holdings, LLC — [3D Systems 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000162828026015997/ddd-20251231.htm):
  - Agreed September 2025, closed 2025-10-31
  - $3.3M in cash plus "a revenue-based royalty receivable which had a present value of $7.1 million", paid in installments 2027-2031
  - Gain of $13.9M
  - Total 2025 gain on dispositions: $139.6M (Geomagic $125.7M plus Oqton/3DXpert $13.9M)
  - Neither divestiture treated as discontinued operations
- Hubb is "a strategic investment group led by principals Steve Lokam and Kalyan Yenneti". 3D Systems continues to sell and support 3DXpert "through our relationship with Hubb Ventures". The stated rationale was to focus development on 3D Sprint, citing AI's potential impact on additive manufacturing — [Metal AM](https://www.metal-am.com/hubb-to-acquire-3d-systems-oqton-and-3dxpert-software-platforms/); [TCT Magazine](https://www.tctmagazine.com/3d-systems-to-divest-oqton-3dxpert-software-platforms/); [3D Systems 10-Q Q3 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/910638/000162828025048915/ddd-20250930.htm)
- The Q4 2025 guidance (VectorShift summary) included about a **$1.2M** revenue and about a $1M gross-margin impact from "the divestiture". This most likely refers to Oqton/3DXpert in November-December, since Geomagic's quarterly contribution was about $7M — [VectorShift Q3 2025 summary](https://vectorshift.ai/research/companies/3d-systems/earnings/2025-q3-summary.html)
- Cimatron and GibbsCAM: announced 2020-11-03 at $65M, closed 2021-01-01 at about $64.2M after adjustments — [Nasdaq/3D Systems press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/3d-systems-announces-sale-of-cimatron-and-gibbscam-businesses-2020-11-03); [Metal AM](https://www.metal-am.com/3d-systems-completes-sale-of-cimatron-and-gibbscam-businesses/)

### Inferences
- An exit of about $10M for Oqton/3DXpert suggests these assets were worth far less than Geomagic, probably because they were subscale or loss-making. Geomagic's cash-generative, flat profile is what made it the saleable "crown jewel" of 3D Systems software.
- The Q4 2025 run-rate impact of about $1.2M for two months implies Oqton/3DXpert revenue of very roughly $7M a year. This is low confidence and depends on how "the divestiture" in the summary is read.

### Gaps
- No disclosed revenue for Oqton/3DXpert at sale.
- The 2021 Oqton acquisition price (3D Systems' cost) was not verified in this session, so the size of the write-down cannot be stated.
