# 3D Scanner Installed Base and Annual Shipments by Price Tier (input for bottom-up sizing of AI scan-to-CAD), as of November 2026

Conventions used throughout:
- **Disclosed** = a number printed in a company filing, a campaign page or a company press release. **Estimate** = my triangulation; the math is shown.
- FX is an **assumption**, not sourced: about ¥7.2 per US$ (rough 2024–25 average) and HK$7.78 per US$ (peg band).
- Price tiers follow the brief: **Tier A** is under $2k (consumer/prosumer), **Tier B** is $2k–$30k (professional), **Tier C** is over $30k (metrology). Vendor categories do not map neatly onto these tiers; each case is flagged below.
- Primary filings were downloaded and read in full text: the Shining 3D BSE prospectus, the Scantech 2025 annual report and the Creality HKEX listing document. Page references are to those documents.

## 1. How many 3D scanners are sold per year, and what is the installed base, by price tier?

### Takeaway
Units are dominated by the under-$2k tier, and the only third-party unit series for it is CIC's (in Creality's filing): **186k consumer 3D scanners shipped worldwide in 2024**, up from 39k in 2020, at about $572 GMV per unit. Adding company-disclosed units gives my estimates for 2025. Tier A is about **220–240k units/yr**. Tier B ($2k–$30k) is about **15–40k units/yr**. Tier C (over $30k metrology) is about **10–20k units/yr**. Estimated **active installed base at end-2026**: Tier A **0.5–0.7M** units (about 1.0M shipped cumulatively since 2020), Tier B **0.1–0.25M**, Tier C **0.07–0.2M**. Only Tier A's annual figure rests on a published unit series. Tiers B and C are built bottom-up from Shining 3D and Scantech unit disclosures plus assumptions, so treat them as low-confidence.

### Cited Findings
**Tier A: consumer (CIC, via Creality's HKEX filing)**

CAGR was 31.5% for 2020–24 and is forecast at 17.1% for 2024–29E. — [Creality HKEX listing document, 29 May 2026, Industry Overview p.87](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0529/sehk26030901313.pdf)
- 2024 consumer scanner share by GMV, per CIC: Creality about $40M (37.6%); "Company E" about $30M (28.2%), described as founded in China in 2014, focused on 3D scanners and 3D cameras, private; "Company F" about $15M (14.1%), founded in China in 2015, focused on 3D scanners and automated imaging systems, private. The top three held 79.9% of GMV. — [Creality HKEX listing document, pp.87–88](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0529/sehk26030901313.pdf)
- **Global consumer 3D scanner shipments: 39,000 units in 2020 and 186,000 units in 2024 (47.8% CAGR).** The top three held 83.5% of shipments, and Creality was #1 with 37.7% by shipments. 36Kr reports these figures from Creality's 2025 prospectus. They do not appear in the May 2026 final listing document, which gives GMV only. — [36Kr (EN)](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3434757526982278); [Kr-Asia](https://kr-asia.com/creality-gears-up-for-hong-kong-ipo-as-it-looks-beyond-3d-printing)
- Creality's own disclosed scanner volumes: **24.0k units (2023), 72.1k (2024), 85.6k (2025)**. Scanner revenue was RMB41.5M, RMB207.6M and RMB365.7M; ASP was RMB1,731.7, RMB2,878.8 and RMB4,273.6. ASP rose because more sales went direct at retail price, and because the RaptorX/Raptor Pro launched at an MSRP above RMB10k. — [Creality HKEX listing document, Business p.142 and Financial Information](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0529/sehk26030901313.pdf)
- Creality says its scanners "are generally designed for a two-to-three-year lifespan under standard use conditions". It procures them from "specialized suppliers" who build to Creality's specs. The Otter series is listed at **US$599–1,000**. — [Creality HKEX listing document, Business p.137](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0529/sehk26030901313.pdf)

**Tiers A/B/C: Chinese vendor unit disclosures** (detail in Q2)
- Shining 3D industrial (non-dental) 3D scanner units sold: **13,106 (2022), 20,207 (2023), 17,461 (2024), 7,031 (H1 2025)**. - Historical anchor for the high end: Creaform sold its **1,000th** handheld scanner unit in May 2009 (a VIUscan, to the Natural History Museum, London). — [Quality Magazine](https://qualitymag.com/articles/86546-creaform-sells-1-000th-handyscan-3-d-laser-scanner)

### Inferences
**A. Annual shipments by tier, 2025 (estimates)**

| Tier (retail price) | 2025 units/yr (est.) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A: under $2k (Creality, Revopoint, 3DMakerpro, Einstar, low-end Shining/Scantech) | **~220–240k** (2026E ~270–300k) | CIC 186k (2024) grown by Creality's +18.7% unit growth → 221k, or by CIC's +23.9% GMV growth → 230k. Add a margin for Einstar-class units that may sit outside CIC's "consumer" bucket. 2026E applies CIC's +27% GMV growth. |
| B: $2k–$30k | **~15–40k** (low confidence) | See build below |
| C: over $30k metrology (portable laser, tracking, arms; stationary fringe projection; CT excluded) | **~10–20k** (low confidence) | See build below |

- **Tier A cross-checks:**
  - 186k × 37.7% = 70.1k, which matches Creality's disclosed 72.1k.
  - CIC GMV per unit: $106.4M / 186k = **$572**, firmly within Tier A.
  - Top three = 83.5% × 186k = 155k units, so Company E plus Company F ≈ 85k units, and all other brands ≈ 31k.
  - Company E is very likely Revopoint (founded 2014, makes scanners and 3D cameras). This is an inference: CIC does not name it.
- **Tier B build (2025):**
  - Shining 3D "professional-grade": about 15.5k units in 2024 at about ¥11.2k (~$1.6k) ex-distributor. Roughly a third to half are probably EinScan-class units above $2k retail, so about 5–8k units. The rest are Einstar-class and belong to Tier A.
  - Scantech 3DeVOK professional: 1,343 units at about ¥19.8k (~$2.7k).
  - Lower-priced metrology-class units from Shining and Scantech priced under $30k: about 1–3k.
  - Artec: about €25M revenue (Craft.co figure in the existing competitors note). At about €15–30k per scanner, that is roughly 0.8–1.5k units.
  - Creaform Go!SCAN/Peel 3D, Revopoint's top end, FARO Freestyle, Thor3D, Polyga and others: unknown, perhaps 5–25k.
  - Sum ≈ **15–40k**.
- **Tier C build (2025):**
  - Scantech industrial: 2,342 units at about ¥147k (~$20k) ex-distributor, including accessories. Retail is typically $30–60k.
  - Shining metrology-grade: about 1.9k units in 2024 at about ¥91k (~$12.6k) ex-distributor. Part of this is retail-priced above $30k.
  - Scantech's annual report says its share of China's domestic industrial handheld/tracking market is at "the head" (top tier). Creaform (AMETEK; about C$150–200M sales in 2021–22 per the competitors note), ZEISS/GOM, Hexagon, FARO and Kreon are each plausibly similar to or larger than Scantech in the Western market.
  - Approximate: about 4k units from the two Chinese vendors plus perhaps 2–4× that from Western vendors gives **~10–20k units/yr**.
- **Active installed base at end-2026 (estimate)** = annual shipments × useful life:
  - Tier A: life is 2–3 years (Creality's own statement). Shipments for 2024–26E are 186k + ~225k + ~285k ≈ **0.70M**, so active ≈ **0.5–0.7M units**. Cumulative shipments 2020–26E (interpolating CIC's 47.8% CAGR for 2021–23: 39k + 58k + 85k + 126k + 186k + 225k + 285k) ≈ **1.0M**. Unique owners are fewer than units because of upgrades and multi-unit buyers. Applying an assumed 15–25% overlap gives about **0.4–0.6M owners**.
  - Tier B: assume a 5–7 year life. 15–40k/yr × 5–7 ≈ **75–280k** (central about 150k).
  - Tier C: assume a 7–10 year life (Artec's 93%-still-in-use claim supports long lives). 10–20k/yr × 7–10 ≈ **70–200k**.
- **Implications for staging:** Tier A has about 10× the unit volume of B and C combined but about $570 GMV per unit. That fits the founder's per-part or low-subscription model. It also means Stage 1 economics depend on what share of owners actually do CAD work (Q5), not on the hardware count.
- **IDC data:** IDC's "635 million units" cannot be right. $3.0bn / 635M = $4.7 per unit. It is almost certainly 635 *thousand* units, which gives about $4.7k per unit. That figure is stale (a 2020 forecast) and conflicts with CIC's 126k consumer units in 2023. IDC's "stationary" category must include many cheap desktop or turntable devices. Do not use it for tiering.

### Gaps
- No public unit series for Tier B or C globally. Creaform/AMETEK, ZEISS/GOM, Hexagon, FARO and Artec disclose no scanner unit volumes. The Tier B and C ranges are bottom-up estimates.
- CIC does not publicly define "consumer 3D scanner" by price. It is unclear whether Shining 3D's Einstar falls in CIC's consumer bucket or outside it.
- No source found for consumer scanner shipments for 2021–2023 (only GMV). The interpolation assumes a smooth CAGR.
- No data on secondary-market or idle units, or on the ratio of owners to units.

## 2. Company disclosures: units or revenue by product line

### Takeaway
Three Chinese filings give hard unit data.
- **Creality**: 85.6k scanners in 2025 at about $590 ASP.
- **Shining 3D**: about 17.5k industrial (non-dental) scanners in 2024, of which roughly 1.9k metrology-grade and 15.5k professional-grade by my decomposition. It also sold 22.4k dental scanners, which are irrelevant to scan-to-CAD.

Revopoint offers only marketing claims ("200,000+ users"). Artec, Creaform and FARO disclose no units.

### Inferences
- **Decomposition of Shining 3D industrial units** (my math, solving units × ASP = revenue for two unknowns; ASPs are rounded to ¥100, so about ±3% error):

  | Year | Metrology-grade units | Professional-grade units |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2022 | ~720 | ~12,380 |
  | 2023 | ~1,370 | ~18,840 |
  | 2024 | **~1,930** | **~15,530** |
  | H1 2025 | ~860 | ~6,170 |

  - Example, 2024: (34,928 − 1.12 × 17,461) / (9.09 − 1.12) = 1,929.
  - Metrology units are growing fast: about 2.7× from 2022 to 2024.
  - Professional-grade units fell about 18% in 2024, consistent with share loss to Creality and Revopoint at the low end (inference).
  - The professional-grade ASP of about ¥11.2k is an ex-distributor price (~$1.56k). Retail is higher, so this line straddles the $2k boundary between Tiers A and B.
- **Scantech implied 2024 units:** industrial 2,342 / 1.0597 ≈ 2,210; professional 1,343 / 2.9978 ≈ 448.
- **Scantech implied ASP (2025, including accessories):** industrial ¥344.76M / 2,342 ≈ **¥147k (~$20k)**; professional ¥26.55M / 1,343 ≈ **¥19.8k (~$2.75k)**.
- **Creality 2025 ASP:** about ¥4,274 (~$590), so Creality is squarely Tier A.
- At about 1 scanner per 10 printers, Creality's 5.7M-user Creality Cloud is the largest addressable funnel visible for Stage 1. A plugin or partnership there would reach scanner owners directly. This is an inference; no data shows scanner owners' activity on Creality Cloud.
- Scanner hardware is migrating toward 3D-printer brands: Creality procures through ODM suppliers, and Bambu Lab is now partnering with Scantech. Over 2026–28, Tier A unit growth may come from printer-ecosystem users with weaker CAD skills.

- Artec, Creaform (AMETEK), ZEISS/GOM, Hexagon and FARO: no scanner unit counts or customer counts published for 2024–2026.

Revopoint's Kickstarter campaigns total roughly **23k+ backers and about $14–15M** across the campaigns found. ### Inferences
- **Sum of disclosed backers:** 6,440 + ~2,400 + 4,911 + 2,923 + 2,798 + 3,300 ≈ **22.8k**. POP 2, POP 3 and INSPIRE backers are unknown, so the true total is probably about 30k.

### Gaps

## 4. Mobile, LiDAR and photogrammetry scanning app users, and the engineering share

### Takeaway
Phone capture reaches millions of downloads, but paying users number in the low hundreds of thousands. Polycam had about 100k paying customers (Feb 2024) and about $6.5M gross revenue in 2023 (Appfigures estimate). No source splits app users by engineering vs. AEC/hobby/media. Phone-capture accuracy is not tolerance-grade, so the engineering-relevant share is probably small.

### Cited Findings
- Polycam raised an $18M Series A in Feb 2024. It had about **100,000 paying customers**, a Pro tier of about $100/yr, and had been cash-flow positive for months in 2023. — [TechCrunch, 7 Feb 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/07/3d-scanning-app-polycam-gets-backing-from-youtube-co-founder)
- Appfigures estimates of Polycam gross consumer spending: about $280k in its first full year, **over $1.8M (2022)**, **$6.5M (2023)**, and over $4M in 2024 year-to-date at end-May 2024. May 2024 was the first month above $1M. — [Appfigures, 31 May 2024](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20240531?f=5)
- Polycam on Google Play: 1M+ downloads (Android only; iOS is Polycam's main platform). — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ai.polycam)
- KIRI Engine on Google Play: 1M+ downloads. AppBrain estimates about 960k Android downloads. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kiriengine.app&hl=en); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/kiri-engine-3d-scanner-app/com.kiriengine.app)
- Scaniverse (Niantic Spatial) is free with on-device processing. A KIRI comparison page is the source for this, so it is a competitor claim. — [KIRI blog](https://www.kiriengine.app/blog/comparison/kiri-engine-vs-scaniverse)
- RealityScan (Epic) is free for entities with under $1M annual revenue; no user counts were found. — [3D Mag app roundup](https://www.3dmag.com/3d-scanners/best-3d-scanning-apps/)
- Third-party "template" sites claim Polycam has 1.2M users or 2M MAU and $31–46.5M ARR. They contradict each other and are unsourced, so they are **not credible**. — [businessmodelcanvastemplate.com](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/blogs/target-market/polycam-target-market)

### Inferences
- **Paying mobile-scan users:** about 100k at Polycam (2024) plus perhaps a few tens of thousands at KIRI and others gives an estimate of **~150–250k paying phone-scan users worldwide**. Downloads are in the millions across apps; iOS counts were not found.
- **Engineering share:** Polycam's marketing emphasizes AEC (floor plans, LiDAR room scans), e-commerce and VFX. If 5–10% of paying mobile users capture mechanical parts (an assumption with no data), that is **~10–25k** potential users for phone-to-CAD. Phone-capture use cases would need "snap-to-standard-feature" logic, because the input is low-accuracy.
- Polycam's 2023 revenue of about $6.5M at about $100/yr implies roughly 65k paying-equivalent users. That is the same order as the 100k figure (annual vs. monthly plans and promotions blur it).

### Gaps
- No official 2025–2026 user or paying counts for Polycam, KIRI Engine, Scaniverse or RealityScan.
- No survey or vendor statement splitting mobile-scan users into mechanical engineering, AEC, hobby or media.

## 5. What share of scanner buyers need scan-to-CAD (vs. 3D printing replicas, art, inspection)?

### Takeaway
I found **no survey** of scanner owners by use case. Market-research summaries mostly name **quality control/inspection** as the largest application by revenue; Allied names reverse engineering as the top segment for 2020. The share of low-cost scanner owners who need parametric CAD is unmeasured. Indirect signals (vendor positioning, bundling) suggest it is a minority in Tier A (perhaps 10–25%) and larger in Tier B. In Tier C, inspection dominates over RE.

### Cited Findings
- IMARC segments the 3D scanning market by application: reverse engineering, rapid prototyping, quality control/inspection, face/body scanning and other. It names **quality control/inspection as the largest**. — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/3d-scanning-market)
- Allied Market Research says the **reverse engineering segment was the highest revenue contributor in 2020**. — [Allied Market Research](https://www.alliedmarketresearch.com/3d-scanning-1421-market)
- Zion says quality control/inspection had the largest share. A secondary excerpt gives it 38.2% of revenue over the forecast period (provenance unclear). — [Zion Market Research](https://www.zionmarketresearch.com/market-analysis/3d-scanning-market)
- Creality's filing positions consumer 3D scanning as "a critical entry point to the 3D creative industry". It lists uses in education, healthcare, metaverse, manufacturing, product design and game modelling, including "developing digital models of existing components in product development" (i.e., RE). — [Creality HKEX listing document, Industry Overview p.87 and Business p.137](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0529/sehk26030901313.pdf)
- Scantech describes the consumer scanner market as moving "from professional-tool attributes to mass consumer goods". It names users in culture and creative work, schools and home education, personal creation, and small studios ("small B and some C users"), with high price sensitivity. It calls the scanner the "data entry point" for consumer 3D printing. — [Scantech 2025 Annual Report, p.16 and p.18](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-28/1225211713.PDF)
- Shining 3D defines its professional-grade line as being for "professional 3D modelling and design" and its metrology line as being for "high-precision industrial 3D measurement and inspection". — [Shining 3D BSE prospectus, p.1-1-87](https://www.bse.cn/disclosure/2025/2025-12-31/1767184813_256643.pdf)
- RE software is bundled with low-cost scanners, which signals demand. Revopoint resells QUICKSURFACE and Design X Go, and Creality sells QUICKSURFACE combos. — see existing note `competitors-pricing-market-size.md` ([Revopoint QUICKSURFACE](https://global.revopoint3d.com/pages/support-quicksurface); [Creality combo](https://store.creality.com/au/products/scanner-quicksurface-combo))

### Inferences
- **Tier A:** Creality and Scantech frame the buyer as a 3D-printing hobbyist or creator. Their scanners are printer accessories (about 1 per 10 printers at Creality). Most scans are likely replicas, figurines, cosplay or art. **Assumption: 10–25%** of owners have a real CAD need (replacement parts, fixtures, aftermarket fit), based on QUICKSURFACE bundling and the "product development" use in the filings. On 0.4–0.6M owners, that gives **~40–150k potential Stage 1 users**. This is the largest uncertainty in the model.
- **Tier B:** Buyers are SMB manufacturers, service bureaus and design studios. Shining calls the category "professional 3D modelling and design". **Assumption: 30–50%** do RE or scan-to-CAD. On about 75–280k active units, that gives **~25–140k seats**.
- **Tier C:** Inspection is the primary use (market reports). **Assumption: 20–35%** of units are also used for RE. On about 70–200k units, that gives **~15–70k**. Penetration here is gated by incumbents (Design X, PolyWorks|Modeler, Creaform Scan-to-CAD) and by regulatory and validation needs.
- **Summary of potential users by stage** (all estimates; ranges compound several assumptions):

  | Stage | Device base (end-2026) | Assumed CAD-need share | Potential users |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1: under $2k plus phone | 0.4–0.6M owners, plus ~150–250k paying phone-scan users | 10–25% (scanner); 5–10% (phone) | **~50–175k** |
  | 2: $2k–$30k | 75–280k units | 30–50% | **~25–140k** |
  | 3: over $30k | 70–200k units | 20–35% | **~15–70k** |

  Stage 1 adds about 40–150k scanner owners and about 10–25k phone users.
- A cheap validation step: survey Revopoint, Creality or 3DMakerpro community forums and Kickstarter backers on "what do you do with your scans?". Alternatively, ask a scanner OEM for its QUICKSURFACE or Design X Go attach rates.

### Gaps
- No primary survey of scanner owners' use cases found (Aniwaa, All3DP, Revopoint and Creality publish none that I found).
- No QUICKSURFACE, Design X Go or Mesh2Surface attach-rate data from scanner OEMs.
- Market-research application shares are paywalled. Their excerpts are by revenue, not units, and conflict.

## 6. 3D scanner market reports with price-tier or handheld segmentation: credibility

### Takeaway
The **only usable unit series is CIC's consumer data in the Creality filing**. It was prepared for an IPO, is reviewed by sponsors, and is consistent with Creality's audited unit sales. Frost & Sullivan's Shining 3D figure is for a broad "3D vision products" market. IDC's 2020 price-band forecast is stale and contains an obvious unit error. Generic SEO-style reports (handheld $1.6bn, 3D scanner $1.3–7.2bn) disagree by up to 5× and give no unit data publicly. Use them only as wide bounds.

### Cited Findings
- **CIC (China Insights Consultancy)** in Creality's filing: consumer scanner GMV $106.4M (2024) and $167.5M (2026E); shipments 186k (2024). Credibility is **medium-high**: it is commissioned for the IPO, but it matches Creality's disclosed 72.1k units. — [Creality HKEX listing document](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0529/sehk26030901313.pdf); [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3434757526982278)
- **Frost & Sullivan** in Shining 3D's prospectus: "3D vision products" at ¥23.38bn (2024) and ¥39.18bn (2029E). Credibility is **medium**, and the scope is broad: it includes dental and other 3D vision. — [Shining 3D BSE prospectus](https://www.bse.cn/disclosure/2025/2025-12-31/1767184813_256643.pdf)
- **IDC (2020)**: price bands <$5k, $5k–$50k and >$50k; "635 million units" in 2023 with about $3.0bn value. The unit figure is internally inconsistent (implies $4.7 per unit). Credibility is **low for units** and the data is stale. — [Digital Engineering 24/7](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/idc-forecasts-strong-growth-for-the-3d-scanner-market)
- **Global Market Insights**: handheld 3D scanners at about $1.6bn (2024), with volume reported in "million units" behind a paywall. Credibility is **low**: the revenue looks 2–3× too high against the bottom-up vendor totals. — [GMI](https://gminsights.com/industry-analysis/handheld-3d-scanners-market)
- **QYResearch**: 3D scanner market of $3,738.7M (2024), with sales volume in "K units" (paywalled). Credibility is **low to medium**. — [QYResearch listing via Sharefair](https://markets.sharefair.com/en/detail/QY0914614488FR6-Global-3D-Scanner-Market-Size-Manufacturers-Supply-Chain-Sales-Channel-and-Clients-2024-2030)
- Other estimates (Coherent MI $2.26bn, KSI $2.34bn, Insight Partners $4.95bn, Emergen $4.68bn, Report Prime $1.33bn, all for 2025) are already compiled in `competitors-pricing-market-size.md`. — [Coherent MI](https://www.coherentmi.com/industry-reports/3d-scanner-market); [Report Prime](https://www.reportprime.com/3d-scanner-r7117)

### Inferences
- **Bottom-up revenue sanity check for 2025** (estimates; FX ¥7.2):

  | Component | 2025 revenue (est.) |
  |---|---|
  | Consumer GMV (CIC 2025E) | ~$132M |
  | Shining industrial (2024 ¥349M ≈ $49M, growing) | ~$50–60M |
  | Scantech total (¥371M) | ~$52M |
  | Artec | ~$27M |
  | Creaform, ZEISS/GOM, Hexagon, FARO, Kreon and others (metrology-grade scanning hardware; large uncertainty) | perhaps $0.6–1.2bn |

  The total for dedicated object scanners (excluding terrestrial LiDAR, dental and CT) comes to about **$0.9–1.5bn**. That sits at the low end of the $1.3–7.2bn published range. Higher estimates likely include terrestrial and long-range laser scanners (AEC and survey) and services.
- For sizing, use CIC for Tier A units and company filings for Tiers B and C. Do not use SEO report CAGRs.

### Gaps
- Full CIC unit series for 2021–2023 and CIC's tier definition are not public.
- No credible public source gives units for handheld metrology scanners (Creaform/Scantech/Shining FreeScan class) or stationary fringe-projection scanners (ZEISS ATOS etc.).
- No 2024–2026 IDC or CONTEXT scanner tracker data found. CONTEXT tracks 3D printers, not scanners.
