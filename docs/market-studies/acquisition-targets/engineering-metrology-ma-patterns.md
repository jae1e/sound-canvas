## Key Question 1: Which small or early-stage companies did these acquirers buy in 3D geometry, reality capture, metrology/inspection, CT, digital twins, AI for engineering, and CAD/BIM tooling?

### Takeaway
Between 2019 and 2026, Hexagon was by far the most active buyer of small 3D, metrology and reality-capture technology companies. Its pattern runs from very small tuck-ins (CAD Service, 9 people; IconPro, about €2M revenue) through mid-size software product companies (Volume Graphics, about €25M revenue and 160 staff; Geomagic, about $30M revenue and 100 staff, for $123M). It also pruned hard: it sold Design & Engineering (simulation), CADS Additive, AICON and Tesa PMI, and spun off Octave. Other acquirers made fewer, platform-driven tuck-ins in this space: FARO (HoloBuilder, GeoSLAM, SiteScape), Matterport (Enview), Epic (Capturing Reality, Sketchfab, Quixel), Niantic (6D.ai, Scaniverse), Bentley (Cesium) and AMETEK (FARO).

### Inferences
- Hexagon is the buyer that most closely matches the founder's thesis. Between 2019 and 2026 it bought CT analysis (Volume Graphics), scan-to-CAD and inspection (Geomagic), handheld scanners (ZG), AI for metrology assets (IconPro), automated reality-to-digital-twin modeling (LocLab), AR/visual positioning (Immersal), and NDT/CT hardware (Waygate).
- Owning Geomagic since April 2025 means Hexagon will not buy a second generic scan-to-CAD or inspection package. A target must add something to Geomagic, Volume Graphics, ZG or Waygate, not duplicate them.
- Acquirers prune deals that stop fitting the core within about 3 years: CADS Additive was bought in 2023 and sold in 2026; CADLM was bought in 2021 and sold in 2026 with D&E. "Core fit" matters more than "nice technology."
- With 3D Systems out of scan software, the independent scan-to-CAD and inspection vendors have consolidated into hardware owners: Hexagon (Geomagic), AMETEK (Creaform plus FARO), ZEISS (GOM). Remaining buyers who might want such a capability include AMETEK, Sandvik, Nikon, Siemens, PTC/Schneider, Autodesk and Bentley. This is an inference from the deal pattern.

## Key Question 2: What patterns show up in deal size, revenue at acquisition, partner or platform fit, and geography?

### Takeaway
There are two distinct tiers.
- **Technology tuck-ins:** 5 to about 50 people, under about €5–10M revenue. Price undisclosed and described as "no significant impact on earnings." They are usually already partnered with the acquirer or integrated into its platform.
- **Product or revenue tuck-ins:** €20–60M revenue and 100–160 staff, bought for about $35–550M. Multiples are about 2–2.5x revenue for hardware-heavy metrology, about 4x for perpetual-license scan/inspection software (Geomagic), and about 10x for subscription engineering software (Rocscience, D&E).

Targets are mostly European (Germany, France, Nordics, UK, Belgium, Italy, Slovakia). Asian targets are rare but do occur (Korea's Rapidform in 2012, China's ZG staged 2021–2026).

## Key Question 3: Which Korean or Asian 3D or engineering software startups have global firms acquired?

### Takeaway
Verified precedents are few.
- Korea's Rapidform (INUS Technology) was bought by 3D Systems for $35M in 2012. Its product line, Geomagic Design X, is now Hexagon's after the 2025 Geomagic deal.
- China's ZG Technology was bought by Hexagon in two stages (51% in 2021, 100% in 2026) and made Hexagon's "centre of excellence for portable metrology."
- Korea's Medit (3D scanners) was sold to private equity rather than a strategic buyer.

### Cited Findings
- **Rapidform (Seoul) → 3D Systems.**
  - Announced about 9–10 Oct 2012 for $35M cash, with about $15M expected revenue contribution in 2013.
  - Rationale: point clouds into parametric CAD; inspection on the production floor; up to 100 new resellers; a boost in Japan and South Korea.
  - Abe Reichental called it an "investment," not a synergy play. — [Geo Week News / SPAR3D, 10 Oct 2012](https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/3d-systems-buys-rapidform/); [3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/3d-systems-acquires-rapidform-1899/)
- **Rapidform/Geomagic lineage → Hexagon (2025).** The Geomagic suite Hexagon bought includes Design X and Control X. — [Hexagon release](https://inderes.dk/releases/hexagon-acquires-inspection-and-3d-modelling-software-capabilities-from-3d-systems)
- **ZG Technology (Wuhan, founded 2014) → Hexagon.** 51% in Aug 2021, remaining 49% on 7 Sep 2026. Hexagon will run one handheld scanning platform and sell ZG scanners outside China. — [Hexagon Cision, 7 Sep 2026](https://news.cision.com/hexagon/r/hexagon-completes-acquisition-of-zg-technology,c4391958)
- **Medit (Korea; founded 2000 by Prof. Minho Chang).**
  - Started with industrial 3D scanners and software, then moved into dental in 2008.
  - 2018 sales were KRW 32.8B (+90% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 10.3B.
  - The 2019 sale of a stake above 50% drew nine PE bidders (KKR, Carlyle, Affinity, TA Associates and others). Strategic investors were excluded. Some bidders valued it at about KRW 600B. — [KED Global, 10 Sep 2019](https://www.kedglobal.com/newsView/ked201909100001)
- **Hexagon's China and Asia exposure.** Hexagon disclosed a 1.198% stake in Unitree, the Chinese humanoid maker, with an expected non-cash gain of about €500M (19 Aug 2026). Its Q3 2026 CEO letter credits "our China export strategy" and "very strong growth in Japan." — [Hexagon Cision, Unitree](https://news.cision.com/hexagon/r/hexagon-s-shareholding-in-hangzhou-yushu-technology-co--ltd--unitree-technologies-,c4385333); [Hexagon Q3 2026 report](https://mb.cision.com/Main/387/4413836/4335597.pdf)
- **Hexagon sold Tesa PMI to China's Hangzhou Great Star (Dec 2023).** Asian companies also appear as buyers of non-core Western metrology assets. — [Hexagon Cision, 19 Dec 2023](https://news.cision.com/hexagon/r/hexagon-agrees-sale-of-non-core-business-area,c3895706)
- **Pype (Bengaluru-based construction AI) → Autodesk, Jul 2020**, undisclosed amount. — [Wikipedia: Autodesk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodesk)

### Inferences
- The Korean precedent (Rapidform) and the Chinese one (ZG) show strategic buyers paying for Asian engineering teams. Both brought (a) a differentiated product already selling globally, or (b) a lower-cost R&D and manufacturing base plus local market access.
- ZG's staged structure (majority stake first, full buyout five years later) is a template a Korea-based company could aim for with Hexagon or with ZEISS, AMETEK or Nikon.
- Medit shows that Korean 3D-scanning success can also exit to PE at high valuations when the market is large (dental). Strategics were explicitly excluded by the seller.

### Gaps
- I could not search Korean press (search budget exhausted) for 2015–2026 acquisitions of Korean 3D, CAD or metrology startups by global strategics.
- Unverified background:
  - Medit's later PE ownership changes (Unison Capital about 2019; MBK Partners about 2022–2023).
  - Korean firms such as Virnect (AR, IPO) and MIDAS IT (CAE) are not known to have been acquired.
  - No verified Japanese 3D or metrology software startup acquisitions by these acquirers.

## Key Question 5: What profile of company would a strategic acquirer such as Hexagon buy? (all inference)

### Takeaway
All of this section is inference from the findings above. The best fit for a Hexagon-type buyer is a small (10–50 person), AI-heavy software company. It should automate a labor-intensive step between a sensor's raw output (scans, CT volumes, point clouds) and a decision (pass/fail, CAD model, digital twin, maintenance action). It should already run on and sell into the acquirer's hardware base through a partner/OEM relationship, and ideally have recurring revenue of €1–10M.

### Cited Findings
This section is analytical. Each inference below is grounded in findings already cited in Key Questions 1–4.

### Inferences
1. **Complement Geomagic, Volume Graphics, ZG and Waygate; do not compete with them.** Hexagon now owns scan-to-CAD and inspection (Geomagic, since Apr 2025), CT analysis (Volume Graphics, since 2020), handheld scanning (ZG, 100% since Sep 2026) and CT/X-ray hardware (Waygate, since Nov 2026). Candidate gaps:
   - (a) AI that automates scan-to-CAD feature recognition and parametric reconstruction beyond what Design X does.
   - (b) AI defect detection and segmentation on CT/X-ray volumes that spans Waygate hardware and VG software. A newly merged CT stack needs automation.
   - (c) Fusing handheld, CMM and CT data into one inspection result and digital twin.
   - (d) Using metrology data for process control or closed-loop manufacturing.
   - (e) Making scan data simulation- and robot-ready (semantic meshes, world-model inputs) to support autonomy and AEON.
2. **Sizing (two tiers):**
   - Technology tuck-in (IconPro, CAD Service, LocLab, Immersal): fewer than 50 people and under about €5M revenue; price undisclosed, likely tens of millions of euros at most.
   - Product tuck-in (Geomagic, Volume Graphics): about €25–30M revenue and 100–160 people; about 4x revenue for license-based software, more for recurring.
   - A founder optimizing for acquisition can aim for tier 1 within 2–4 years. Tier 2 requires building a real global product business.
3. **Integrate first, then get acquired.** The most repeatable Hexagon path (CAD Service: partner since 2013; LocLab: prior partnership; ZG: 51% stake first) is to become a partner, plug-in, OEM component or minority-investee. Ship on the acquirer's file formats and SDKs, sell through its channel, and be embedded in a share of its customers before any approach. Equivalent hooks elsewhere: Bentley iTwin / Cesium 3D Tiles, Epic Fab and Unreal, FARO Sphere, AMETEK/Creaform VXelements, ZEISS (GOM) software, Autodesk APS.
4. **Recurring revenue raises the multiple.** Recurring software trades around 10x (Rocscience at about 10.7x, D&E at about 10.2x). Perpetual-license and hardware-adjacent businesses trade around 2–4x (Creaform, Rapidform, Waygate about 2.3x; Geomagic about 4.1x).
5. **Strategic fit has to last.** Hexagon sold CADS Additive about 3 years after buying it, and CADLM and AICON left in the 2026 portfolio clean-up. A target must sit in the acquirer's stated core (for Hexagon: precision measurement, positioning, autonomy) to avoid being re-sold.
6. **A Korea base can work if it is an advantage.**
   - Precedents show strategics buy Asian teams when the team is a cost-effective R&D center or brings access to Asian OEMs: Rapidform for Japan/Korea reach, ZG as the "centre of excellence."
   - A Korean startup could pitch Korean shipbuilding, semiconductor, battery and automotive OEMs as reference customers. Hexagon reports strong growth in aerospace & defence, electronics and Japan.
7. **The buyer pool beyond Hexagon has also consolidated:**
   - AMETEK (Creaform plus FARO) now owns two scanner-software stacks and may need unification or AI software.
   - ZEISS owns GOM.
   - Bentley, Autodesk and Epic buy reality-capture-to-platform technology.
   - Niantic Spatial and Matterport/CoStar operate in the capture-to-digital-twin market.
   - Building on a vendor-neutral format (E57, 3D Tiles, OpenUSD, STEP AP242 PMI) keeps several of these buyers in play.
8. **Founder-specific signal:** The founder's former product line (Design X / Control X) changed hands in 2025 at about 4x revenue with about 100 staff, suggesting acquirers see it as a mature cash business. New, AI-native capability on top of that base is more likely to command a strategic premium than another general-purpose scan-processing tool.

### Gaps
- The profile relies heavily on Hexagon data. Verified deal-level evidence from Siemens, Dassault, PTC, Autodesk, ZEISS, Nikon, Sandvik and AMETEK on *small* 3D/metrology tuck-ins is thin, so the cross-acquirer profile should be treated as a hypothesis.
- No disclosed prices exist for sub-€5M-revenue technology tuck-ins in this set, so price expectations for an acqui-hire-stage company remain unquantified.
