# Assistive-AI Workflow Applications Outside Manufacturing Quality (as of November 2026)

Scope: application areas outside manufacturing quality/compliance that fit the founder's thesis:
(1) AI assists and a human reviews or edits the output; (2) the task is tied to the customer making money; (3) the task is tedious, or the person doing it keeps changing, or it is infrequent enough that nobody becomes fluent; **(4) consumer-level buyer fit (added mid-study):** an individual practitioner buys and uses it at roughly $10–50/month or a small per-use fee, on a personal card or within an expense limit that needs no procurement approval. Self-employed users and sole proprietors are preferred, because the money gained goes straight to them. Each candidate also carries a **data-upload flag** for cases where confidentiality or regulation would block someone from uploading company data to a personal tool. Constraints: small Korean software company, limited budget, self-service SaaS preferred, editable outputs, no foundation-model training.

Note on criterion 4: one buyer type comes up repeatedly that the original brief did not name. **Solo licensed professionals** (관세사 customs brokers, 행정사 administrative agents, 노무사 labor attorneys, freelance grant or proposal writers) pay for their own tools, and criterion 2 is met through "saving billable expert time." They are bound by professional secrecy, so their data-upload flag is "caution, not blocked."

Research date: 2026-11-03. Tags used on each finding: **[Fact]** (regulator, official notice, or primary document), **[Press]** (news report), **[Vendor claim]** (company's own statement), **[Anecdote]** (practitioner or blog opinion), **[Estimate]** (modelled or projected figure), **[Aggregator/unverified]** (secondary listing I could not confirm against a primary source).

## Q1. Trade/export documentation for SMEs: FTA origin determination, origin verification, HS classification, US tariff-era origin and refund work

### Takeaway
Origin determination is the strongest trade-documentation fit for the thesis, especially for Korean SMEs exporting to the US, Türkiye and the EU, and for their domestic suppliers. Foreign origin audits are infrequent but costly, and since 2025 US tariffs are set strictly by origin. Korea's government hands out free origin consulting and the free FTA-PASS system every year, which shows the capability gap keeps coming back. It also caps what SMEs will pay. The IEEPA refund wave was very large but is a one-time event that has already moved into broker and law-firm processes.

### Cited Findings
**Origin verification volume and patterns (Korea)**
- [Press, 2024-06-13] Foreign customs sent Korean exporters 730 origin-verification requests in 2021, 369 in 2022 and 323 in 2023 (318 FTA-preferential, 5 non-preferential). Türkiye sent 198 of the 2023 requests, the EU 68 and India 18. Most non-preferential requests come from the EU and concern Chinese goods transshipped through Korea. — [Tax Times (taxtimes.co.kr) no.264988](https://www.taxtimes.co.kr/mobile/article.html?no=264988)
- [Press, 2024-06-13] Typical failure modes: (a) fabric that was only dyed in Korea was still declared "KR" under the Korea–Türkiye FTA; (b) origin declarations were issued for HS-6 items the firm was not approved to export; (c) an overseas subsidiary used the parent's approved-exporter number without authorization; (d) the goods met Korea's HS-6 change rule but not the importing country's stricter rule. Consequence: preferential tariff treatment is denied and the firm's credibility suffers. The article says many exporters break the rules because they don't fully understand them. — [Tax Times no.264988](https://www.taxtimes.co.kr/mobile/article.html?no=264988)
- [Press, 2026-05-04] The Korea Origin Information Institute (한국원산지정보원) found that origin questions made up under 1% of US CBP advance rulings in the mid-2010s, started rising after 2019, and reached about 35% in 2025. Under the second Trump administration, origin ("substantial transformation") decides both the tariff burden and whether goods clear customs. In some categories a few components or processes decide origin: PCBAs, filter media, conductors and cores, APIs. — [Tax Times no.274988](https://www.taxtimes.co.kr/news/article.html?no=274988)
- [Press, 2025-03-25] The Korea Customs Service (KCS) announced targeted origin inspections (기획검증) of KORUS-FTA exports in high-risk product groups: items subject to US anti-dumping duties and items the US frequently audits. Firms that made errors get consulting. Firms that falsely certified non-Korean goods face "strict measures." — [Newsseoul](https://newsseoul.co.kr/news/view/1065584415068601)

**Government support (a sign of the gap, and a ceiling on willingness to pay)**
- [Press, 2026-02-02] KCS runs a yearly origin-verification response consulting program: customs brokers (관세사) visit the firm and help with origin documentation, a verification-response manual, a mock verification and FTA-PASS adoption. The consulting value is capped at ₩2M. Firms with prior-year revenue up to ₩50bn pay nothing; up to ₩100bn they pay 20% (max ₩400k); up to ₩150bn, 30% (max ₩600k). US exporters get priority in 2026. In 2025 it supported 364 firms; 243 newly gained Approved Exporter status and 177 adopted an origin management system. — [Tax Times no.273640](https://www.taxtimes.co.kr/news/article.html?no=273640)
- [Fact, 2025 notice] The second-half 2025 program also gave US-bound SME exporters top priority. It has run every year since 2021. — [Yangsan Chamber of Commerce notice (PDF)](https://yangsancci.korcham.net/file/dext5uploaddata/2025/2025년%20하반기%20원산지검증%20대응%20지원사업%20공고문.pdf); program description per [search summary of KCS/press results](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202502171933431255)

**US tariff-driven documentation (2026)**
- [Fact/law-firm summaries, April 2026] The April 2, 2026 proclamation applies Section 232 steel, aluminum and copper duties to the full customs value, including derivatives. Products that are 15% or less metal by weight are excluded. Rates depend on the reported country of melt-and-pour (steel) or smelt-and-cast (aluminum). Unknown smelt-and-cast origin on derivative aluminum defaults to 200%. Importers are told to maintain melt/pour and smelt/cast origin documentation under an Annex III transitional framework that runs through 2027. A June 1, 2026 proclamation reportedly modified the regime again. IEEPA HTS codes stopped applying at 12:00 a.m. EST on 2026-02-24. — [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/supreme-court-overturns-trumps-ieepa-tariffs); [Foley, 2026-03-10](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/what-every-multinational-should-know-aboutmanaging-the-aftermath-of-the-supreme-courts-ieepa-tariff-decision-part-ii-2/)

### Inferences
- **Who does the work today:** Exporters' own trade staff, often one person in a small firm who also handles logistics. Customs brokers (관세사) fill in on a project basis, and KCS consulting covers a few hundred firms a year. That a free program has to run every year (364 firms in 2025) suggests the expertise does not stay in the firms. No direct turnover statistic was found.
- **Why it fits the thesis:** An origin audit arrives without warning, years after shipment. It asks for BOMs, supplier origin confirmations (원산지확인서) and cost build-ups that nobody in the firm has assembled since. That is infrequent, high-stakes and evidence-heavy, which is the ideal profile for criterion 3. A useful product would draft the origin worksheet and evidence pack per FTA or rule (and for US substantial-transformation questions), check supplier declarations, and flag gaps, with a licensed broker or the exporter signing off. That fits criterion 1.
- **Money link (criterion 2):** If an audit fails, the importer loses preferential duty and the buyer relationship suffers. On US shipments, an origin determination now decides whether goods land at a punitive rate. CBP origin rulings rising from under 1% to about 35% of advance rulings shows how central origin has become.
- **Willingness to pay is the weak point:** KCS caps consulting at ₩2M and makes it free for SMEs under ₩50bn revenue, and FTA-PASS is free. A paid product must beat "free plus a broker visit" on speed and audit-readiness. Selling through customs brokers (B2B2B) or to their SME clients may work better than direct self-service.
- **IEEPA refunds:** A large but time-boxed one-off. By November 2026 it is mostly being handled through brokers, CAPE and litigation. It is not a durable SaaS wedge, though "refund readiness" checks for small importers could have been a short-lived niche.
- **Section 232 melt-and-pour content declarations:** Korean exporters of steel- and aluminum-containing derivatives probably have to feed their US importers metal-content and melt/pour data. This is recurring and tedious, and it decides duty rates of 100–200%. It borders the other researcher's "material certs" scope, so coordinate before pursuing it.
- **Consumer-level buyer fit (criterion 4):** **Medium** overall. It is **High** for two buyer types. (a) Owner-operators of micro exporters: one-person trading firms and small online sellers shipping B2B under an FTA, where the owner signs the origin declaration and pockets the duty saving. (b) Solo 관세사 who do origin consulting: KCS pays brokers up to ₩2M per firm visit, so a ₩20–50k/month tool that halves dossier preparation saves billable time. It is **Low** when the user is an employee of a mid-sized exporter, because procurement and IT policy apply. Pricing should be per dossier (for example ₩10–30k per origin worksheet or verification pack) or a monthly practitioner seat.
- **Data-upload flag: Caution.** Origin worksheets need BOMs, supplier prices and cost build-ups, which are trade secrets. An employee uploading them to a personal tool would typically breach company confidentiality policy. Owners uploading their own data are fine. Customs brokers handling client data carry statutory secrecy duties (관세사법). No regulation was found that bans cloud processing outright (gap).
- **Scores:** Origin determination and verification response: Assistance **High**, Money **High**, Fluency/turnover **High**, Consumer fit **Medium** (High for owner-operators and solo 관세사). Low WTP against free government help is the main risk. IEEPA refunds: Assistance **Medium**, mostly mechanical CSV work; Money **High**; Fluency **High**, one-off; Consumer fit **Medium** (a per-use or contingency fee could suit sole-proprietor importers of record); but transient. HS classification for SMEs: Assistance **High**, Money **Medium** (it affects duty indirectly), Fluency **Medium**, Consumer fit **Medium–High** (cheap per-lookup use by small sellers; product descriptions are not sensitive). Most SMEs classify a stable catalogue once. Section 232 metal-content declarations: Consumer fit **Low** (B2B mill and importer data flows).

### Gaps

- Origin-verification counts for 2024–2025, and US-specific counts: not found. The latest total found is 2023 (323).

## Q2. EU border-sustainability rules for non-EU exporters: CBAM (definitive period), EUDR, Battery Regulation

### Takeaway
CBAM became financially live on 2026-01-01, but the cash bite in 2026 is small: 2.5% of embedded emissions at about €75/t. The real pressure comes later: verified actual data from 2027, default-value mark-ups of 10% (2026), 20% (2027) and 30% (2028+), a proposed downstream extension from 2028, and a phase-in reaching 48.5% in 2030 and 100% in 2034. The obligation sits with the EU importer, but the data work falls on Korean producers. Korean customers (Hyundai, Samsung, LG) already demand supplier emissions data. The thesis fits (annual, English-language, specialist, SMEs lack staff), but the space is crowded with CBAM SaaS, and Korea's subsidy program bundles metering hardware with software. EUDR and the Battery passport fit Korean SMEs poorly. On criterion 4, CBAM scores Low: the buyer is the plant, prices are company-level, and installation data is confidential.

### Cited Findings
**CBAM rules and timeline**
- [Fact] The definitive regime applies from 2026-01-01. It covers cement, iron and steel, aluminum, fertilizers, electricity and hydrogen. Importers need authorization, file reports, and surrender certificates priced off EU ETS auctions. — [European Commission, CBAM](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en)
- [Fact/certification body] A cumulative 50 t-per-importer-per-year threshold replaced the €150-per-shipment exemption. It covers steel, aluminum, cement and fertilizer; electricity and hydrogen stay in scope regardless. Once the threshold is crossed, the whole year's imports are in scope. Authorised Declarant applications were due 2026-03-31. The first annual declaration for 2026 imports is due 2027-09-30. From 2026, actual emissions may be used only if verified by an accredited third-party verifier; otherwise Commission defaults under IR (EU) 2025/2621 apply. Legal liability rests with the EU importer, but compliance depends on non-EU producers' data. — [BSI](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/insights/blogs/preparing-for-eu-cbam-the-20262027-transition-explained); see also [Malta CBAM authority](https://climateaction.gov.mt/cbam/)
- [Press, 2026-02-03] Certificate sales start February 2027, and the quarterly holding requirement fell from 80% to 50%. Official verification starts in 2027, and Korea plans to build domestic verifier capacity. The trade ministry formed a cross-ministry CBAM task force on 2026-01-21. An industry quote: "This year is quiet, but next year the receipts will pile up." — [Hankyung ESG](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202601225893i)
- [Press, 2026-05-11] Default values carry mark-ups of 10% in 2026, 20% in 2027 and 30% from 2028. In December 2025 the Commission proposed extending CBAM to downstream steel and aluminum goods from 2028. All reporting is in English, and SMEs lack calculation experience and staff. Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics suppliers effectively must provide emissions data on request, and Hyundai Motor makes emissions submission a purchasing condition. — [Industry Ilbo (kidd.co.kr) 245968](https://kidd.co.kr/news/245968)
- [Fact/NGO report; the cover says July 2025 but the content cites April–May 2026 events, so it is probably July 2026] The first official CBAM certificate price was €75.36/tCO₂, announced 2026-04-07. CBAM phase-in factors: 2.5% (2026), 5% (2027), 10% (2028), 22.5% (2029), 48.5% (2030), 100% (2034). Korea gives steel free allocation through 2030, so K-ETS payments do not currently qualify for the CBAM carbon-price deduction. — [For Our Climate (기후솔루션), "보이지 않는 탄소 청구서" (PDF)](https://content.forourclimate.org/files/research/dAdfNbf.pdf)
- [Estimate, same report] Steel-related CBAM cost for Hyundai's Czech plant could be ₩40.2bn and for Kia's Slovak plant ₩42.7bn, or up to ₩87.1bn and ₩92.4bn with penalties. Hyundai disclosed ₩2.1bn through 2030; Kia disclosed nothing. These figures illustrate how much defaults versus actual data matter. — [For Our Climate (PDF)](https://content.forourclimate.org/files/research/dAdfNbf.pdf)

**Korean government support (shows what a package costs)**
- [Fact, MSS notice 제2026-248호, 2026-04-06] The "2026 SME CBAM Response Infrastructure (MRV)" program funds about 20 SME manufacturers of CBAM goods exporting, or planning to export, to the EU. The cap is ₩60M per firm at a 70% subsidy (₩42M). Breakdown: metering hardware ₩20–40M; software for energy monitoring and CBAM reporting ₩15M; verification ₩5M. The "standard" software-plus-verification package is ₩20M, of which the government pays ₩14M. The program's verifiers are official Korean verifiers but **not EU-accredited**. Selection includes a written business plan, a CEO pitch and a site visit, followed by settlement (정산). — [MSS notice (PDF)](https://grant-documents.thevc.kr/297141_%5B%EC%A0%9C2026-248%ED%98%B8%5D_2026%EB%85%84_%EC%A4%91%EC%86%8C%EA%B8%B0%EC%97%85_CBAM%EB%8C%80%EC%9D%91_%EC%9D%B8%ED%94%84%EB%9D%BC%EA%B5%AC%EC%B6%95_%EC%82%AC%EC%97%85_%EC%B0%B8%EC%97%AC%EA%B8%B0%EC%97%85_%EB%AA%A8%EC%A7%91%EA%B3%B5%EA%B3%A0.pdf); [Digital Daily, 2026-04-05](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2026040514194842804)
- [Fact] Regional programs also exist: Changwon Chamber's 2026 carbon-border-tax support (April–December 2026), and Gyeongnam's 2025 emissions-calculation program with an EU-accredited verifier opinion, aimed at auto, aviation and appliance parts makers responding to customer data requests. — [Changwon Chamber notice (PDF)](https://changwon.korcham.net/file/dext5uploaddata/2026/2026년%20탄소국경세%20대응%20지원사업%20참여기업%20모집공고.pdf); [Financial News, 2025-06-10](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202506101016526321)

**Competitors / funding**
- [Press, 2025-07-24] Glassdome (글래스돔, Korea) offers SaaS CBAM management (product identification, process monitoring, automatic calculation, report generation, a link to verification) and says LRQA confirmed it meets EU requirements. **EUDR**
- [Fact/secondary] Amending Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 (published 2025-12-23) moved main obligations to 2026-12-30 for medium and large operators and to 2027-06-30 for micro and small operators. Downstream operators no longer file their own due diligence statements. Micro and small primary operators file a one-time simplified declaration. ### Inferences
- **Who does the work today:** In Korean SMEs, the plant manager or a quality/environment person, usually with a consultant or a subsidized package. Reporting is annual, in English, and the methodology keeps changing (Omnibus, defaults, downstream proposal). That matches criterion 3.
- **Money link:** In 2026 it is indirect but real. Suppliers without verified actual data push EU importers onto marked-up defaults and lose on price. Big Korean OEMs make emissions data a purchasing condition. The direct cost rises sharply after 2028: 10% phase-in plus 30% default mark-up, then 48.5% in 2030. Score: **Medium in 2026, High by 2028–2030.**
- **Assistance fit:** **High.** Plant data comes in, an editable emissions report and EU communication template comes out, and an accredited verifier signs. The software line in the MSS package (₩15M, 70% subsidized) gives a Korean price anchor. A self-service product that drafts the EU supplier communication, checks data completeness, and prepares the verifier evidence binder fits a small team. Metering hardware does not.
- **Risks:** The space is crowded (EU and Korean vendors). Rules are in flux. EU-accredited verification capacity for Korean plants is limited: the MSS program's verifiers are not EU-accredited. The 50 t threshold removes many small EU importers, but that does not reduce Korean producers' duty to supply data to large importers.
- **EUDR:** Korean SME exposure is mostly rubber and wood derived products, plus downstream traders who no longer file. Fit for a Korea-based founder is **Low–Medium**. It fits better for commodity exporters in producing countries.
- **Battery passport:** Mainly a large-company problem (cell and pack makers). **Low** fit for self-service SMEs. Not sourced this session.
- **Consumer-level buyer fit (criterion 4): Low.** The buyer is the plant: an SME manufacturer of steel, aluminum or fasteners whose purchases go through the company. Reference prices are company-level: a ₩20M "standard" software-plus-verification package and ₩15M software in the MSS program. The person doing the work is an employee (plant, quality or environment staff), not a sole proprietor, and the money gained (keeping the EU or OEM buyer) goes to the company. A narrow exception is independent CBAM consultants and verifiers as practitioner buyers, who could save billable time with a personal tool. But that market is small and those buyers are price-insensitive at a higher tier.
- **Data-upload flag: Caution to Blocked.** Installation-level energy, process and production data is commercially sensitive. Customers such as Hyundai, Samsung and LG may impose supplier confidentiality on data shared for their requests. An employee uploading it to a personal tool would likely breach policy.
- **EUDR / Battery passport consumer fit: Low.** Both are corporate supply-chain obligations.
- **Scores for CBAM supplier data packs:** Assistance **High**, Money **Medium→High**, Fluency/turnover **High** (annual, English, rules changing), Consumer fit **Low**.

### Gaps
- Number of Korean firms, and SMEs, exporting CBAM goods to the EU, and the export value: not found. A KIEP estimate of over ₩300bn a year for steel appeared in a search snippet, but I could not pin it to a specific article.
- Market prices for CBAM verification of a Korean installation by an EU-accredited verifier: not found. The MSS program's ₩5M is for a non-EU-accredited verification opinion.

## Q3. Supplier questionnaires: ESG ratings (EcoVadis/CDP/customer ESG) and security/RFP questionnaires

### Takeaway
Customer-imposed questionnaires are clearly tied to winning and keeping customers, and they are annual and tedious. But security and RFP questionnaires are a crowded, well-funded category. ESG questionnaires for small suppliers come with low prices (EcoVadis itself runs about €449–2,869 a year for suppliers). Building it as a standalone product looks weak. Bundled with CBAM or product-carbon-footprint data for Korean suppliers it could be stronger.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact/secondary, October 2026] EcoVadis supplier plans in France: Basic €449–1,949/yr and Premium €889–2,869/yr depending on company size. Medals require Premium or higher. Internal preparation time is extra. — [Projet Celsius](https://projetcelsius.com/en/faq/bilan-carbone/combien-coute-ecovadis/)

## Q7. Product registration for consumer-goods exporters: K-beauty (FDA MoCRA, EU CPNP and others)

### Takeaway
This is a strong, fast-growing fit. MoCRA registrations and listings grew about 15% and 31% in six months during 2026. The duties are per SKU and recurring: listing within 120 days, annual listing updates, biennial facility renewal, 15-business-day adverse-event reports, safety substantiation. Amazon makes compliance proof a condition of selling, so the revenue link is immediate. Small K-beauty brands with many SKUs and a one-person export team are the textbook "many hats, changing rules" customer. Korean law firms already sell MoCRA services, which shows willingness to pay.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact, FDA] Manufacturers and processors register facilities and renew every two years. The "responsible person" (manufacturer, packer or distributor named on the label) lists each product and updates listings annually. There is a small-business exemption, except for eye-area, injected, internal-use and long-lasting (over 24h) products. The FDA issues no registration certificates. As of 2026-06-30: 16,398 active facility registrations and 1,298,361 active product listings. — [FDA Registration & Listing](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/registration-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products)
- [Vendor blog] As of 2026-01-06: 14,299 active facility registrations and 992,907 active listings. The Cosmetics Direct portal has shown renewal dates and sent reminders since 2026-02-11. Amazon asks for FDA registration details or FEI, a GMP certificate and a Certificate of Analysis in many beauty subcategories before listing or keeping products live. A lapsed registration is a prohibited act. A fragrance-allergen labeling proposed rule was expected in May 2026, and the GMP rule is a long-term action. Brands with hundreds of SKUs need a single source of truth for ingredient lists and labels. — [Pattern](https://www.pattern.com/blog/mocra-for-beauty-brands-selling-on-amazon)
- [Law firm] Covered foreign facilities must name a US agent. New facilities register within 60 days of starting manufacturing for US distribution. Products are listed within 120 days of first marketing. Serious adverse events are reported within 15 business days with a label copy. Safety substantiation is required, and supplier documentation alone is not enough. Businesses averaging under $1M a year in US cosmetic sales are exempt from registration, listing and GMP, but not from safety, labeling or adverse-event duties. A Korean-linked firm (Daeryun) sells MoCRA compliance services. — [Daeryun Law US compliance](https://us-comp.daeryunlaw.com/practices/detail/mocra-compliance)

### Inferences
- **Growth signal:** From January to June 2026, active listings rose from about 993k to 1.30M (+31%) and facilities from 14.3k to 16.4k (+15%). That comes from comparing the Pattern and FDA figures, which use the same FDA series.
- **Who does the work:** Brand owners' overseas-sales or regulatory staff (often one person), contract manufacturers (ODMs such as Kolmar or Cosmax register facilities), and US agents, consultants and law firms. Multi-market work (US MoCRA, EU CPNP and Responsible Person, UK SCPN, China NMPA, ASEAN notifications) means expertise rarely covers every market.
- **Scores:** Assistance **High**: the AI drafts INCI lists in SPL format, label compliance checks per market, safety-substantiation dossiers and adverse-event report drafts, and the responsible person signs. Money **High**: a listing gap means Amazon delisting or a border hold, and new markets open new revenue. Fluency/turnover **High**: per-SKU and per-market rules change (allergens, GMP) and small brands have thin teams.
- **Product shape:** A "regulatory dossier from one product master" SaaS that turns one SKU master (formula, INCI, label artwork) into per-market submissions and checklists. It suits LLM/VLM work (reading label images, INCI normalization) plus rule logic. It borders the other researcher's "chemical compliance" scope (ingredient restrictions), so coordinate.
- **Consumer-level buyer fit (criterion 4): Medium–High.** The natural buyer is the owner of a small K-beauty brand or a one-person cross-border seller (Amazon, Shopee, TikTok Shop). They are the labelled "responsible person," the revenue gated by Amazon proof requests is theirs, and per-SKU work maps to a per-use fee (for example per listing, per label check, per adverse-event draft) or a ₩20–50k/month plan. Two caveats lower it from High. (a) Sellers under $1M average US sales are exempt from registration and listing, though not from safety, labeling or adverse-event duties, nor from Amazon's proof requests. That shrinks the per-SKU listing workload at the smallest end. (b) Larger brands' regulatory work is done by employees or by the ODM (contract manufacturer), which are company purchases.
- **Data-upload flag: Low for label and INCI work, Caution for full formulas.** INCI lists and label artwork are public on the pack. Full quantitative formulas and safety-test data are trade secrets, often owned by the ODM rather than the brand. Uploading them may breach ODM supply contracts. Design the product so listing and labeling work needs only INCI and label data, and treat formula-level safety substantiation as an optional, owner-only feature.

## Q8. HR/labor compliance for Korean SMEs, especially foreign-worker (E-9) administration

### Takeaway
The pain is real: complex procedures, two agencies (Employment Center and Immigration), and many small manufacturers relying on E-9 workers. But many filings are form-filling that could be fully automated, and the money link is indirect (keeping the workforce, avoiding sanctions). It is a medium-fit candidate. It works better as a feature in an HR or 행정사 workflow than as a standalone product.

### Cited Findings
- [Press, 2025-04-16; KBIZ survey of 1,177 firms using the Employment Permit System] 86.8% are satisfied with the system. Top complaints: complex application procedures (31.6%), long wait to worker arrival (22.1%), the workplace-change system (12.3%). The Employment Center and Immigration Office split reduces efficiency for applications such as re-employment. 61.7% of dissatisfaction with workers is about language. — [Kyeongbuk Maeil](https://kbmaeil.com/article/20250415500238)
- [Press, 2026-01-11; KBIZ 2025 survey of 1,223 SMEs employing foreign workers] 82.6% hire foreign workers because domestic job-seekers are short. Average monthly cost per worker is ₩2.53M (₩2.93M with meals and lodging). The top management difficulty is communication (52.1%). — [Gyeonggi Ilbo](https://www.kyeonggi.com/article/20260111580241)
- [Press, 2025-08-08] E-9 workers can change workplaces at most three times in three years. 68% of EPS employers (2023 KBIZ survey) had received contract-termination demands for workplace changes. The labor ministry was considering easing change rules. — [Daum/News](https://v.daum.net/v/20250808082756475)
- [Vendor content] HR SaaS (Shiftee) publishes guides on foreign-worker hiring reports and contracts, a sign that HR tools are absorbing this. — [Shiftee guide](https://shiftee.io/ko/blog/article/guide-to-foreign-worker-hiring-reporting-contracts)

### Inferences
- **Scores:** Assistance **Medium**: many tasks are deterministic forms, which leans toward replacement, while multilingual worker communication such as contract and safety-rule translation is assistive. Money **Low–Medium**: it keeps production staffed, but no revenue is gated per filing. Fluency/turnover **High**: small factories' admin staff rotate, and filings are event-driven (re-employment, workplace change, stay extension).
- An adjacent and possibly stronger Korean angle is multilingual safety, work-instruction and contract generation for foreign workers. It addresses the 52.1% communication pain and Serious Accidents Punishment Act exposure (not researched here).
- **Consumer-level buyer fit (criterion 4): Medium.** Two buyer types fit. (a) Owners of very small factories and farms who handle EPS paperwork themselves. They would pay ₩10–30k a month personally, but the money link for them is indirect: keeping staff, avoiding sanctions. (b) Solo 행정사 or 노무사 who file EPS and stay paperwork for many small employers. For them it saves billable time, which strengthens criterion 2 to **Medium**.
- **Data-upload flag: Caution.** Inputs are workers' passports, alien registration numbers, visas and wage records, which are personal data under PIPA, some of it unique identifiers. The employer or agent is the data controller. Using a cloud AI service requires a proper processing-consignment (처리위탁) disclosure and safeguards. An employee using a personal account would breach it. Owners and licensed agents can comply if the vendor offers a consignment-ready data-processing agreement.
- **Scores:** Assistance **Medium**, Money **Low–Medium** (owner) / **Medium** (solo 행정사), Fluency **High**, Consumer fit **Medium**.

Cross-cutting design note: ranks 1–4 share a primitive. Each takes one owner-held master record (business plan and budget; SKU and INCI master; product, BOM and supplier master; company profile and past proposals) plus public rules or a public RFP, and produces an editable, per-regime evidence dossier with flagged gaps that the individual signs.
